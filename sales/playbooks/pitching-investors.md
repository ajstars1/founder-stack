# Pitch investors and other powerful, time-starved people
**Use when:** an investor meeting, a board, or an executive buyer who interrupts, rushes you, or drags you into the weeds.  ·  **Sources:** Pitch Anything (Klaff), Never Split the Difference (Voss), The Challenger Sale (Dixon), Founding Sales (Kazanjy)

## Method
1. **Grade your evidence before you write a slide.** Give every traction claim its ledger ID and grade. Investors probe exactly where the grade is weak, so know it first. Scores come from `/startup-scorecard`, economics from `/business-model-canvas`.
2. **Fit it in about 20 minutes, in four phases** [Klaff]:
   - **~5 min, who and what.** One or two track-record wins, then why now. Name the economic, social and technology shifts that opened this window. Then the one-sentence idea.
   - **~10 min, how it works.** Your plan and how you'll spend the money (not a hockey stick), the competition, how easy it is to enter and to switch, and your unfair advantage.
   - **~2 min, the deal.** What they get, the terms, the timing, and what you need from them.
   - **~3 min, the close.** Stack the frames below so they want in before they start analyzing.
   Say up front that you'll take about 20 minutes. Attention fades after that, and holding to it signals discipline [Klaff].
3. **Say the idea in one sentence:** for [customer] who is dissatisfied with [current option], we offer [new category] that [key benefit], unlike [alternative]. Klaff borrows this from Moore's positioning template [Klaff]. The positioning itself comes from `/gtm`.
4. **Choose your frames and keep them true** [Klaff]:
   - **Prize:** you are choosing investors too, so they should qualify themselves.
   - **Time:** a real round close date.
   - **Intrigue:** a short story about something *you* did, with risk and stakes. Stop it at the cliffhanger when a drill-down starts, and finish it later.
   - **Mission:** values that are hard to argue with.
   A fake deadline or fake demand gets found out, and then nothing else you say is believed.
5. **Handle derailers** [Klaff]:
   - **Status plays** (keeping you waiting, "I have ten minutes"): answer with light humour and a small refusal to be rushed.
   - **The detail spiral:** give the top-line number, offer the data room, return to fit.
   - **Fading attention:** wrap up early, before they check out.
6. **Kill neediness.** Don't fish for approval or ask "so, what do you think?". State the ask, stop, and be ready to leave [Klaff].
7. **If you lead with insight,** use the teaching arc from `demo-and-pitch.md`: a reframe they haven't considered, backed by data only you hold [Dixon].
8. **Negotiating terms or giving bad news** (a slow quarter, a down round): open with an accusation audit, use labels, and ask calibrated how/what questions. Never split the difference on terms you care about [Voss].

## Klaff or Voss?
Klaff's frame control works when you hold something scarce: a competitive round, real traction, a deadline that exists. Without leverage, status games read as arrogance. Default to Voss's approach of letting them feel in control while your questions steer [Voss]. Klaff himself warns that nobody enjoys being dominated [Klaff].

## Rules of thumb
- One great point beats five good ones. Listeners average impressions rather than adding them up [Klaff].
- Show your spending plan, not revenue fantasies [Klaff].
- Never pitch in a noisy café or on a trade-show floor [Klaff].
- Grades persuade: "4 paying pilots (E4)" beats "huge interest".
- The same rules fit executive buyers. Keep the founder narrative structure for the content [Kazanjy].

## Mistakes
- Hour-long decks that bury the ask.
- Arguing logic when an investor challenges your frame. Hold it calmly instead.
- Inventing urgency.
- Presenting assumptions as facts. Unsourced market sizes cost credibility.
- Asking for feedback at the end instead of a decision or next step.

## Validate it
- **Assumption created:** "Investors with thesis T will fund us at stage S on current evidence" (`A#`, `[ASSUMPTION, E0]`).
- **Cheapest test:** 8–10 first meetings with a targeted list. Track each one's outcome, then fix the weakest phase before the next batch.
- **Evidence bar:** "keep us posted" is E0. Detailed questions are E1–E2. A partner meeting, data-room access or diligence calls are E3, and a term sheet is a strong E3. Money in the bank is E4. Fundraising market facts (round sizes, valuations) need `[FACT — url]` via web search.
