# Run a pipeline you can forecast from
**Use when:** the pipeline is a wish list, deals sit for months, or you need to say what will close this quarter (for the board, hiring or cash).  ·  **Sources:** Gap Selling (Keenan), Founding Sales (Kazanjy), Predictable Revenue (Ross), The JOLT Effect (Dixon), SPIN Selling (Rackham)

## Method
1. **Define your own stages.** The default CRM stages and percentages describe someone else's sale [Kazanjy]. Make every exit a buyer action, not a seller activity. Example:
   - Meeting held
   - Qualified: the four yeses (`discovery-calls.md`)
   - Gap agreed in numbers
   - Decision process mapped
   - Proposal requested
   - Verbal yes
   - Contract out
   - Won / lost / disqualified
2. **Every open deal carries a dated next step that the buyer committed to.** "I'll follow up" doesn't count. If there's no such step, it's a stall: close it out or win a real next step [Keenan] [Rackham] [Kazanjy].
3. **Review weekly,** biggest deals nearest to close first, a few minutes each. For each deal, answer seven questions [Keenan]: Do we know their current state? Their future state? The gap in numbers? Why they personally care? How they'll decide? What evidence do we hold? What happens next, and when?
4. **Run the notes test.** Could a colleague name the account from its anonymised notes? If not, the deal isn't understood [Keenan].
5. **Score indecision on large deals,** and report no-decision losses separately from competitive ones [Dixon].
6. **Check coverage.** Pipeline needed is roughly the target divided by your win rate. A $300k quarter at a 25% win rate needs about $1.2M qualified [Keenan]. Track lead sources separately, because referrals, broad marketing and targeted outbound convert at different rates and speeds [Ross].
7. **Forecast in three buckets.** *Commit*: the seller owns it and will be held to it. *Best case*: plausible upside. *Pipeline*: everything else. Question a commit hard, then accept the owner's number; never inflate it. A low but honest commit is a gift, because it shows the hole while there's time to fill it [Keenan].
8. **Track a few metrics** [Ross] [Keenan]:
   - new qualified pipeline per month (a board-level number);
   - stage conversion;
   - win rate;
   - cycle length;
   - deal size;
   - bookings, split into new, expansion and renewal.
9. **When a number moves, walk the tree.** Is it deal count or deal size? Win rate or opportunity volume? Activity? Then listen to the calls behind the branch that moved [Kazanjy].
10. **Work closest to the money first.** Late-stage deals get your hours before new prospecting does [Kazanjy].

## Rules of thumb
- If the commit lands within about ±15% of actuals for several periods running, the forecast is real [Keenan].
- A deal with no buyer action in 30+ days is likely dead. Run the gone-dark play or close it [Kazanjy].
- Close-lost freely and keep good notes, then re-engage stuck deals after about two months [Kazanjy].
- Pipeline from the founder's network is not repeatable pipeline. Tag the source.
- Before about 20 closed deals, a win rate is a direction; quote it with the sample size.
- "If it isn't in the CRM, it didn't happen" [Ross]. A shared spreadsheet is fine early.

## Mistakes
- Stages named for what the seller did ("demo given").
- Forecasting by gut percentage.
- Leaving dead deals open to make the pipeline look big.
- Pushing a team to raise their commit. That only teaches them to pad numbers.
- Mixing renewals into new-business bookings.

## Template
```
STAGES (exit = buyer action)
| Stage | Exit criterion (buyer did…) | Typical days | Conversion to next |

DEAL CARD: <account>, <amount>, <stage>, source: referral / inbound / outbound / network
Current state ☐ Future state ☐ Gap $____ ☐ Why they care ☐ Decision criteria ☐
Evidence held (E-grade): ____   Next buyer action: ____ on <date>
Indecision risk: low / med / high   Bucket: commit / best case / pipeline

WEEKLY FORECAST
| Week | Commit | Best case | Pipeline | Coverage (pipe ÷ gap-to-target) | Won | Lost (decision / no decision) |

MONTHLY ACCURACY
| Month | Commit | Actual | Error % |
```

## Validate it
- **Assumptions created:** "our win rate is W% and our cycle is D days" (`A#`) and "the forecast is accurate within ±15%" (`A#`, `[ASSUMPTION, E0]`).
- **Cheapest test:** rebuild win rate and cycle time from CRM or inbox records, not memory. Then log commit against actual each month.
- **Evidence bar:** numbers from records become facts (`F#` with the CRM export as the source); remembered numbers stay E0–E1 assumptions. Within a deal, its grade is its latest buyer action: a scheduled step is E3, and cash is E4. Forecast accuracy is validated after three consecutive periods within band.
