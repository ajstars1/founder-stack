# Turn friendly conversations into honest buying signals
**Use when:** prospects keep saying "love it" but nobody commits, or you're moving from learning conversations to asking for the sale. For full interview guides and debriefs, use `/customer-discovery`. This playbook covers the seam where learning meets selling.  ·  **Sources:** The Mom Test (Fitzpatrick), SPIN Selling (Rackham), Never Split the Difference (Voss), The Lean Startup (Ries)

## Method
1. **Know which mode you're in.** In learning mode you don't pitch and don't ask; in selling mode you pitch, then ask. Blending the two corrupts both: buyers start being polite instead of truthful, and you start hearing what you want [Fitzpatrick].
2. **Bring your three big questions.** Before each batch of conversations, agree with your team on the three things you most need to learn. One should be capable of killing the idea [Fitzpatrick].
3. **Repair bad data live** [Fitzpatrick]:
   - A **compliment** → steer back to what they do today.
   - **Fluff** ("I always…", "I would…", "I might…") → ask when it last happened and what they did.
   - A **feature request** → ask why they want it and how they cope without it.
4. **Check the problem matters.** Do they spend money or hours on it? Is it in their top three? Have they looked for a fix? Someone who has never searched won't buy yours [Fitzpatrick].
5. **Keep early ones short and casual.** A five-minute question at an event beats a scheduled hour. Save formal meetings for when you have something concrete to show [Fitzpatrick].
6. **Reveal the idea late, then ask for something real.** Commitment comes in three currencies: time, reputation and money [Fitzpatrick]. Every meeting either advances or fails. A friendly chat with no agreed action is a stall, the same thing Rackham calls a Continuation [Rackham].
7. **Climb the ladder one rung per meeting.** A follow-up with an agenda → an intro to the boss or budget holder → a trial on their data with written success criteria → a testimonial or case study → a letter of intent → a deposit, pre-order or paid pilot.
8. **Test any yes with "how".** "Sounds great, keep me posted" is a compliment plus a stall. Ask how they'd roll it out and who else must agree. A real yes survives the implementation questions; a polite one evaporates [Voss].
9. **Discount letters of intent.** Ries tells of a team that hired on the strength of signed letters, then found the signers weren't early adopters and real sales were much harder [Ries]. A letter is the weakest money signal.
10. **Hard-to-reach seniors:** ask for advice, not a sale. It lowers their guard and puts you in a learner's seat [Fitzpatrick].

## Rules of thumb
- Decide the commitment you'll ask for before the meeting, not during it.
- Talk less than they do. If you did most of the talking, the meeting produced no data [Fitzpatrick].
- If you catch yourself pitching mid-learning, say so, stop, and ask them to finish their earlier point.
- Ask about money plainly. Early buyers expect a startup to ask.
- Rejection is information; not asking is the failure [Fitzpatrick].
- If 10+ conversations still give scattered answers, your segment is too broad; slice it (`icp-and-prospect-lists.md`).
- Conversations can't remove product risk ("can we build it, will they keep using it?"). Test that with `/experiments`.

## Mistakes
- Asking "would you buy this?" or "what would you pay?". Answers about the future are fiction.
- Counting compliments, waitlist signups or "keep me posted" as pipeline.
- Asking for commitment before you've learned anything, which reads as a pitch.
- Treating feature requests as the roadmap.
- Hiring, or quitting a job, on LOIs alone.

## Template
```
CONVERSATION CARD: <name/role/company>, <date>, mode: LEARN / SELL
My three questions: 1. ____ 2. ____ 3. ____ (the scary one: ____)
Commitment I'll ask for if it goes well: ____   Fallback: ____

REPAIR LINES (own words, adapt)
Compliment → "Appreciate that. How are you handling this today?"
Fluff      → "When did that last happen? What did you do?"
Request    → "What would that let you do that you can't now?"
Polite yes → "If we started next month, how would it roll out? Who else weighs in?"

HOW IT ENDED (circle one)
compliment E0 | stall E0 | told past story E1 | showed workaround/data E2 |
gave time / intro / trial with criteria E3 | LOI (E3, weak) | paid or deposit E4
Next rung + date: ____
```

## Validate it
- **Assumption created:** "Segment S will commit to the next rung after one conversation" (`A#`, `[ASSUMPTION, E0]`).
- **Cheapest test:** ask everyone in the next 10 conversations for the same rung, and record how each ended using the card above.
- **Evidence bar:** compliments and stalls are E0. Past-behaviour stories are E1. Shown workarounds or data are E2. Time, intros, trials with criteria, and LOIs are E3, with an LOI counting as the weakest E3. Money is E4. Working-validated means E3 from ≥3 independent people. Willingness to pay accepts only E4. Before hiring or building heavily on letters, convert them to deposits or paid pilots.
- Debrief each conversation through `/customer-discovery` so the ledger moves.
