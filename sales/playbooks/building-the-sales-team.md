# Hire, onboard and coach your first sellers, and keep the customers they win
**Use when:** deciding whether and whom to hire (SDR, AE, VP Sales, customer success), onboarding or coaching reps, or fixing churn and renewals.  ·  **Sources:** Founding Sales (Kazanjy), Predictable Revenue (Ross), The Challenger Sale / The JOLT Effect (Dixon), Gap Selling (Keenan), SPIN Selling (Rackham)

## Method
1. **Pass the gate before hiring** [Kazanjy]:
   - you can book your own meetings;
   - the narrative holds across accounts;
   - the win rate is steady, somewhere in the 15–30% band;
   - cost of sales can sit around a fifth to a quarter of revenue.
   Before that, a hire inherits an unsolved problem.
2. **Grow in steps** [Kazanjy]: founder alone → founder plus a prospector (or a customer-success hire) → plus two closers → a small pod (prospector, closers, customer success) → more pods.
   - **Hire two closers together.** If a lone rep fails, you can't tell a weak rep from a weak playbook. That inference comes from Kazanjy's two-AE stage.
   - **Specialize before it hurts** [Ross]. When a role spends over a fifth of its time on a secondary job, split that job out. After the first closer, hire a dedicated prospector rather than another closer. One prospector usually feeds two to five closers.
3. **Don't hire a VP Sales first.** A builder of teams can't build the motion they're supposed to scale [Kazanjy].
4. **Hire for traits, not logos.** Look for intellect, a knack for figuring things out, tolerance for grind, and coachability [Kazanjy], plus curiosity and business sense [Keenan].
   - Be wary of reps whose big-brand employer did the selling for them [Kazanjy], and of lone wolves who ignore process [Dixon].
   - In complex sales, prefer people who will challenge a customer over people who mainly please one [Dixon].
   - Former customers and customer-success staff often handle hesitant buyers well [Dixon].
5. **Screen by doing:** a written exercise, a recorded voicemail pitch, a phone screen on funnel maths, a mock discovery call and a stalled-buyer role-play. In reference calls, ask what would make this person a 10 [Kazanjy] [Dixon].
6. **Onboard with a boot camp** of one to two weeks. Cover the market and problem, the narrative, demo certification, the tools, then role-play drills. Graduates get some easy deals and call reviews [Kazanjy]. Certify behaviour on recorded calls, not attendance; most training is forgotten within a month [Dixon].
7. **Pay to the behaviour you want** [Ross] [Kazanjy]:
   - prospectors on qualified meetings that the closer accepts;
   - closers on roughly half base, half variable, with commission paid on cash received.
   - No commission-only reps [Ross].
8. **Coach behaviours, one at a time.** Practise each new skill several times on low-stakes calls before judging it [Rackham]. Spend coaching on the middle of the team, where the gains are largest [Dixon]. Use metrics to find the problem and recorded calls to see it [Kazanjy].
9. **Separate customer success from selling** [Ross]. CS exists to deliver what the sale promised [Kazanjy]:
   - one-to-one onboarding that starts by re-checking the buyer's goals;
   - a written success plan;
   - early usage signals that predict value;
   - quarterly business reviews;
   - an auto-renew clause, with a renewal conversation about six weeks out [Kazanjy].
   Customers pushed to sign while still anxious bring that doubt into onboarding [Dixon].

## Rules of thumb
- Ramp takes one to three months for inside sales and six to eighteen in the field [Ross]. Budget the cash for it.
- If your reps close at lower rates than you do, coach them up rather than taking the deals back. Several decent reps beat one heroic founder [Kazanjy].
- Partner channels follow direct success; they don't create it [Ross].
- Group onboarding sessions leave half-implemented users who churn later [Kazanjy].

## Mistakes
- Hiring a closer to "find out if it sells".
- One generalist doing prospecting, inbound, closing and renewals.
- Paying on bookings that never turn into cash.
- Coaching on outcomes ("close more") instead of behaviours.
- Treating CS as support tickets rather than delivering on what was promised.

## Validate it
- **Assumption created:** "A hired rep using our playbook can close at least X% of qualified deals within N months" (`A#`, `[ASSUMPTION, E0]`). Set X and N before hiring.
- **Cheapest test:** before a full hire, have a contractor or part-time prospector run the sequence for 4–6 weeks.
- **Evidence bar:** meetings they book that a closer accepts are E3. Deals they close are E4. Retention and renewals are E4 from billing records (F#). Hiring cost and payback go to `/business-model-canvas`.
