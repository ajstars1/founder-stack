# Run a discovery call that finds the gap
**Use when:** preparing or reviewing a first sales call, or the buyer admits the problem but feels no urgency.  ·  **Sources:** SPIN Selling (Rackham), Gap Selling (Keenan), The Challenger Sale / The JOLT Effect (Dixon), Founding Sales (Kazanjy), Never Split the Difference (Voss)

## Method
1. **Prepare.** Research the account so you need few background questions; failed calls have more of them [Rackham]. Write one Advance objective: a named person doing a named thing by a date. List three or more problems you solve, each with two or three knock-on effects and a question to surface each [Rackham] [Keenan].
2. **Open briefly.** Introduce yourself, state your purpose, get their consent to dig in, and agree the agenda and a good outcome [Rackham] [Kazanjy]. A warm-up past about a fifth of the call puts you behind [Rackham].
3. **Map the current state** in five layers [Keenan]: facts that bear on what you sell, problems, impact (what drives the sale), root causes, and how people feel.
4. **Grow the need.** Ask about consequences: who else it hurts, what it costs, what it blocks, what happens if it worsens. Implication questions turn a mild complaint into a reason to act [Rackham]. Pin every vague answer: "a lot" becomes how many, how often, in what currency [Keenan].
5. **Build the future state.** What would fixing it mean for the business, the team and them? Get a number. Need-payoff questions make the buyer voice the value, which rehearses your champion's internal pitch [Rackham].
6. **Do the gap maths.** Future-state value minus current state. If the gap isn't bigger than price plus switching pain, dig further or walk [Keenan].
7. **Find the motive.** Why this goal, why now? The real reason is often personal or competitive [Keenan].
8. **Qualify on four yeses** [Keenan]: a problem you fix, they agree they have it, they want it fixed, they'll work through it with you. Strict budget-authority-need-timing checks fail in new categories nobody has budgeted for [Kazanjy]. Map decision criteria, steps, and people not in the room [Keenan] [Voss].
9. **Close the call.** Summarise their situation in their words until they agree you've got it exactly right [Voss]. Then propose the highest realistic next step [Rackham].
10. **Notes test.** If a colleague can't tell which account your notes describe, you haven't learned enough [Keenan].

## Open vs closed questions, settled
Rackham's call research found no measurable link between question form and success; big-deal wins tracked question *type*, implication and need-payoff [Rackham]. Keenan's "ask open, refuse open answers" is the same claim from the other side [Keenan]. So stop counting open vs closed: open prompts get stories ("walk me through the last time…"), closed ones pin numbers and confirm. Judge the call by what you leave with: quantified impacts, stated value, a mapped process. Limit: once a buyer has intent and is stuck, more questions hurt; recommend instead (`stalled-deals-and-indecision.md`) [Dixon].

## Ask or teach?
With a pattern from 10+ similar customers, open with a hypothesis ("firms like yours usually find X; does that match?") and let them correct you [Dixon]. Below that, ask: an insight you can't defend costs more credibility than it buys.

## Rules of thumb
- Never ask need-payoff questions about a need you can't meet; you'd strengthen a reason to buy elsewhere [Rackham].
- Follow heavy implication questions with need-payoff ones so the buyer leaves hopeful [Rackham].
- Listen more than you talk in discovery; won discovery calls lean toward the buyer talking [Keenan] [Ross].
- Redo discovery with every new stakeholder. Never reuse one person's pain in front of their peer [Keenan].
- Above roughly $10k a year, hold discovery and demo on separate days [Keenan]. In small or early deals, fold discovery into the meeting and keep asking throughout [Kazanjy].

## Mistakes
- Pitching at the first sign of pain.
- Leaving without numbers.
- Counting "let's talk again sometime" as progress; it's a stall, not an Advance [Rackham].
- Meeting only the enthusiast and never learning who can veto.

## Validate it
- **Assumption created:** "Problem P costs accounts like this at least $X a year, and fixing it is worth more than our price" (`A#`, `[ASSUMPTION, E0]`).
- **Cheapest test:** ask for the artifact behind the number (report, spreadsheet, ticket count).
- **Evidence bar:** their estimate is E1, their own data is E2, and an agreed next step with the decision group is E3. Working-validated: E2 impact data plus an E3 next step from ≥3 accounts. Log debrief deltas through `/customer-discovery`.
