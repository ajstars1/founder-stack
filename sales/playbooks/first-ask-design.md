# Design an ask a stranger can say yes to
**Use when:** cold messages get no replies, people accept the connection but never answer, or you're about to write a first touch or a follow-up.  ·  **Sources:** Influence (Cialdini), Founding Sales (Kazanjy), SPIN Selling (Rackham), Breakthrough Advertising (Schwartz), The Mom Test (Fitzpatrick); also Never Split the Difference (Voss), $100M Offers (Hormozi), Alchemy (Sutherland), The JOLT Effect (Dixon), Predictable Revenue (Ross)

## Method
1. **Place the reader's awareness first** [Schwartz]. Do they know the problem exists? Do they know fixes exist? Do they know you? Copy built on your product name and offer only works on the most aware. For the problem-aware, open with the problem in their words. For the unaware, open with news or an identity they recognise, and leave out product, price and features.
2. **Decide the Advance before writing** [Rackham]. Pick the biggest step this person could realistically take at this level of trust. From a stranger it's usually a reply. The first message sells a conversation, never the action itself [Kazanjy].
3. **Climb a ladder of asks** [Cialdini]: reply, then look at a sample, then try it on something low-stakes, then use it for real. People who said a small yes accept bigger asks later. Strangers refuse the big ask when it comes first. Don't skip rungs with strangers.
4. **Give before you ask** [Cialdini]. An unrequested, useful gift creates the pull to reply: a finding about their own company, a short teardown, an answer to a question they posted. Don't make them do work to unlock the gift.
5. **Count their steps and cut them to one** [Hormozi]. Every extra click, form, person to involve or wait lowers the odds. "Reply yes" beats "book a slot" beats "send us your data".
6. **Name the worst case and remove it** [Dixon] [Sutherland]. To a busy buyer, acting on a stranger's offer feels riskier than ignoring it, because only action can go wrong in a visible way. Say what can't happen ("nothing goes to your customer", "bin it if it's useless").
7. **Make no easy and safe** [Voss]. End on a question they can turn down without awkwardness, and say the suspicion out loud before they think it ("you probably get several of these a week").
8. **Show effort only they would recognise** [Sutherland]. One detail from their own world (the role they posted, a line from their changelog) is a costly signal that this isn't a blast. Generic inboxes and templates signal the opposite.
9. **Read the response by what it cost them** [Fitzpatrick]. An accepted connection, an open or a like costs nothing, so it predicts nothing. Only replies, time given, intros and money count.

## Rules of thumb
- If touch 1 asks for something a stranger would need a meeting to agree to, shrink it.
- Write one message per awareness stage. Buyers with the problem on their desk today (often intermediaries like recruiters or agencies) need less setup than founders who haven't felt it yet [Schwartz, inference].
- After an accept, send the gift or a one-line question, not the pitch you already sent.
- A free offer that gets no takers fails for one of three reasons: they don't want it, don't believe it, or never saw it [Hormozi]. Test one cause at a time, and give a believable reason why it's free.
- Under 90 words, one question, one credibility fact [Ross].
- Still silent after the full window (`cold-outreach-and-cadence.md`)? The cheapest fix is a warm path: ask people who trust you "who do you know who has this problem right now?" (rings, `first-customers-founder-led-sales.md`) [Fitzpatrick] [Kazanjy].
- [Field] A news hook must survive a fact check by the reader. One wrong detail ("X dropped Y") costs more trust than the hook earns.

## Mistakes
- Asking for the full trial in the first message.
- A gift that needs them to do work first.
- Treating accepts, opens and reactions as interest.
- Pitching features to someone who doesn't yet feel the problem.
- One message for every segment.

## Template
```
ASK DESIGN: <segment>
Awareness: unaware | problem-aware | solution-aware | knows us
Realistic Advance for this touch: ____________ (usually: a reply)
Ladder: reply → sample → low-stakes try → real use. This touch is rung __
Gift (no work needed): ____________
Their steps to say yes: __ (target 1)
Worst case they fear: ____________ → removed by: ____________
Safe-no question: ____________
Detail only they'd recognise: ____________
Opening line by stage:
  unaware: news or identity, no product ____________
  problem-aware: their problem in their words ____________
  solution-aware: why this fix, proof ____________
```

## Validate it
- **Assumption created:** "Segment S at awareness stage W replies to ask K" (`A#`, `[ASSUMPTION, E0]`).
- **Cheapest test:** two asks on adjacent rungs, ~50 accounts each, same list quality and cadence (`cold-outreach-and-cadence.md`).
- **Evidence bar:** accepts and opens are E0. A reply that engages is E2. A reply that takes the next rung (sends the link, books the call) is E3. Working-validated needs E3 from ≥3 independent accounts per ask.
