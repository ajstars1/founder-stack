# Unstick a stalled deal, and know when to stop pushing
**Use when:** a buyer who wanted it has gone quiet, keeps asking for one more demo or reference, says "let me think about it", or the champion can't get it through internally.  ·  **Sources:** The JOLT Effect / The Challenger Sale (Dixon), Gap Selling (Keenan), Never Split the Difference (Voss), SPIN Selling (Rackham), Founding Sales (Kazanjy)

## Method
1. **Diagnose: status quo or indecision?** Have they accepted that today's way must go, and that you're the likely choice [Dixon]?
   - **Unknown** (typical when a buyer goes dark after a demo): re-contact first (step 2) and branch on the reply. A demo that ended without a dated buyer action was a continuation, not progress [Rackham].
   - **No:** the status quo is still winning. Sell the change: revisit the gap, cost the delay, reframe (`discovery-calls.md`, `demo-and-pitch.md`).
   - **Yes:** indecision, and the job flips from proving they'll gain to proving they won't regret it. In JOLT's data it caused more no-decision losses than status quo preference [Dixon].
2. **Gone dark?** Restate the goals they gave you and ask what changed [Keenan], or offer to close the file in a message that's easy to say no to; people reply to keep the option [Voss].
3. **Judge the indecision** [Dixon]:
   - Find its source. **Valuation:** they can't choose between options. **Information:** they feel they haven't researched enough. **Outcome:** they doubt it will work for them.
   - Gauge depth: endless input-gathering, criteria-hopping, chasing the perfect option, pushed dates.
   - Deprioritize the hopelessly stuck and forecast them honestly.
4. **Recommend.** "In your seat, I'd start with X, because…" One option with your personal backing beats a menu [Dixon]. Treats valuation fears.
5. **Limit the research.** Before another demo or reference call, ask what exactly it must confirm and whether that would be enough to decide. Be the expert yourself rather than leaning on specialists [Dixon]. Waiting for complete information is usually delay in disguise. Treats information fears.
6. **Take the risk off the table** [Dixon]:
   - Promise a believable outcome, below your best case.
   - Add downside protection: a cancellation window, a phased rollout, the scariest part carved into a cancellable term, a joint plan with owners and milestones agreed before signature.
   - Start smaller than they asked.
   Treats outcome fears, the hardest of the three.
7. **Act as their agent.** Talk them out of what they don't need, admit gaps, name where a rival is stronger, then ask for the business confidently [Dixon].
8. **Arm the champion, and multi-thread.** Most selling happens inside without you [Rackham]. If the champion goes silent, reach a second contact with a reason of their own (a stakeholder-specific outcome), never to go around the champion; tell the champion you're doing it. A synthesis across sources. Give your contact one page per stakeholder (outcome, likely objection, answer) in their bosses' language [Dixon]. Ask how absent people see it [Voss].
9. **Test intent** with an effortful request, such as convening the decision group within two weeks. Evasion signals avoidance [Dixon].
10. **Let go cleanly.** Close-lost with notes and a re-engage date about two months out [Kazanjy].

## When *not* to push the status quo
Once a buyer has intent, re-selling the "why" backfires: JOLT found reps relitigating on most calls, usually doing more harm than silence [Dixon]. Urgency, scarcity, dwelling on pain and warnings of being left behind all feed the fear that is already the problem, and exploding discounts deepen indecision [Dixon]. Cost-of-delay arguments [Keenan] and Challenger's quantified hidden cost [Dixon] belong *before* intent. After it, lower risk.

## Rules of thumb
- "Next quarter, priorities are shifting" from a buyer who had intent usually means decision avoidance. Find the fear behind it [Dixon].
- Every stalled deal needs a buyer action dated in the calendar, or it isn't in the pipeline.
- Record no-decision losses separately from losses to competitors. They have different cures [Dixon].
- Talk less in discovery; lead more once they're stuck [Dixon] [Keenan].

## Mistakes
- Sending another ROI deck to a buyer who already believes the ROI.
- Inventing a deadline.
- Approving a fourth reference call without asking what it's for.
- Treating "I need to think about it" as neutral. It correlates with losing [Dixon].

## Template
```
STALL DIAGNOSIS: <account>, last buyer action <date>
Accepted change is needed?  Y / N   Chose us?  Y / N
→ N: status-quo play (gap, delay cost, reframe)   → Y: indecision play below

Indecision source (tick): valuation ☐ information ☐ outcome ☐
Depth signals: keeps gathering ☐ criteria-hopping ☐ maximizer ☐ pushes dates ☐
Verdict: pursue / deprioritize / close-lost (re-engage <date>)

RECOMMENDATION: "In your position I'd start with ____ because ____."
RESEARCH LIMIT: "What would that next demo need to show for you to decide?"
RISK REMOVAL: believable outcome ____ | safety net ____ | smaller start ____
CHAMPION KIT: | Stakeholder | Outcome | Objection | Answer |
INTENT TEST: ask ____ to convene ____ by <date>

GONE-DARK NOTE (own words, adapt)
"<Name>, when we spoke you wanted <goal> by <date>. Has something shifted?
If this has dropped off your list, tell me and I'll close it on my side."
```

## Validate it
- **Assumption created:** "Most of our no-decision losses are indecision, not status quo" (`A#`, `[ASSUMPTION, E0]`).
- **Cheapest test:** tag your last 10–20 no-decision losses from emails and call notes. Then run one risk-removal offer on the next three stuck deals.
- **Evidence bar:** tagged records are E2. A convened decision group or a signed joint plan is E3. A signed and paid contract after the offer is E4. Working-validated means E3 from ≥3 stuck deals that moved.
