# Handle objections, and prevent most of them
**Use when:** "too expensive", "not now", "we need to check with IT", "we're fine as we are", a trial request, or "we're looking at a competitor".  ·  **Sources:** SPIN Selling (Rackham), Founding Sales (Kazanjy), Gap Selling (Keenan), Never Split the Difference (Voss), The JOLT Effect (Dixon)

## Method
1. **Prevent before you handle.** Count objections per call. A high rate usually means you offered solutions before building the need, or leaned on generic "this helps you" lines. Fix it in discovery, not with rebuttals [Rackham].
2. **Pre-empt the predictable ones.**
   - Open a hard conversation by naming the worst things they might think about you, before they say them. Said aloud, those charges usually shrink [Voss].
   - When a buyer's acknowledgement turns lukewarm ("I think so"), answer the unspoken doubt there and then [Dixon].
   - Turn any question asked twice into a slide [Kazanjy].
3. **Run the loop** [Kazanjy]:
   - catch the objection;
   - find the question underneath it;
   - answer with proof;
   - confirm it's resolved;
   - return to the next step.
4. **Don't defend; ask.** Label what you hear ("it sounds like the timing worries you"). Then ask what or how: "What would need to change for this quarter to work?" [Voss]. Hold the objection up against the goals they gave you in discovery: "You said fixing X was worth Y. Help me square that with waiting." The buyer then explains the gap, rather than you justifying the product [Keenan].
5. **Respond by type:**
   - **Price/value.** Put the outcome they named next to the price, then stop talking [Keenan] [Kazanjy]. Separate *no budget* from *can't afford*. No budget: look for money already being spent on agencies, renewals, headcount or workarounds [Kazanjy]. Can't afford: walk away, kindly [Keenan].
   - **Timing.** If they haven't yet accepted the need to change, quantify what each month of delay costs [Keenan]. If they already want it, this is indecision; go to `stalled-deals-and-indecision.md` [Dixon].
   - **Authority** ("I need to run it by…"). Map who decides and how. Offer to equip your contact, and ask how the people not in the room are likely to react [Voss].
   - **No need** ("we're fine"). Test the current state again. There may be no gap. Walk, or set a check-back date [Keenan].
   - **Competitor.** Ask how they'll decide, then compare those criteria with the outcome they want. Challenge any criterion that doesn't serve it [Keenan].
   - **Trial request.** Ask what the trial must prove. Offer references, a guided walk-through, or for larger deals a structured pilot. Give it success criteria, an end date, named users and a price that applies if it succeeds. Never an unguided free trial [Kazanjy] [Keenan]. Raise its cost if it's too cheap to signal intent, for example a case-study commitment, team-wide use or a card on file [Fitzpatrick].
6. **Admit true objections.** If you truly can't meet the need, or a competitor is better at it, say so and narrow the deal or walk. Candour earns more trust than a stretched answer [Rackham] [Dixon].

## Rules of thumb
- A rep with far more objections than peers needs discovery coaching, not rebuttal scripts [Rackham].
- Price objections early in a call usually mean features came too soon [Rackham].
- Silence after stating price is part of the answer. Don't fill it [Kazanjy].
- Rebut every voiced objection. Ignored ones resurface at signature [Dixon].
- An objection you hear three times is a narrative bug. Fix the deck, not the rep.

## Mistakes
- Discounting at the first push.
- Arguing feature by feature.
- Answering the stated objection when the real one sits underneath.
- Using fear tactics on a buyer who is already scared of choosing wrong.
- Granting a "quick free trial" with no criteria, owner or end date.

```
Must prove: ____ | Success metric + threshold: ____ | Users: ____

```

## Validate it
- **Assumption created:** for a recurring objection, "objection O stems from cause C (e.g. value unclear, not budget)" (`A#`, `[ASSUMPTION, E0]`).
- **Cheapest test:** tag every objection across the next 10 deals by stage and type. Change one thing upstream, such as discovery questions or the order of proof, and watch whether the rate drops.
- **Evidence bar:** your recollection of objections is E0–E1. Logged words from recordings or notes are E2. A pilot with signed criteria is E3, and conversion at full price is E4. Any price change stays an assumption until refusable prices produce E4 results. Price strategy goes to `/gtm`.
