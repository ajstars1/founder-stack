# Be different or be memorable? Settle differentiation vs distinctiveness
**Use when:** "we look like every competitor", "is our difference strong enough?", someone cites Sharp to argue difference doesn't matter, or a budget fight between a new story and brand consistency.  ·  **Sources:** How Brands Grow (Sharp), Zag (Neumeier), The 22 Immutable Laws of Branding (Ries & Ries), Start With Why (Sinek), The Hero and the Outlaw (Mark & Pearson)

## The conflict, briefly
Sharp's buying data: in established categories few users see their brand as different, rivals share similar buyers, and brands grow by being easy to recall and buy. So build distinctive assets and reach every category buyer. [Sharp] Neumeier, Ries and Sinek: clutter erases sameness, so own a new space, a word or a belief. [Neumeier] [Ries & Ries] [Sinek] Both are right about different jobs. Sharp measured mature repeat-purchase brands; the other camp argues from launch-stage winners, with survivor bias. Neither directly covers a startup nobody has heard of.

## Method
1. **Separate the two layers.** Difference lives in the *offer layer* (what you sell, to whom, against which alternative; `/gtm` decides it). Distinctiveness lives in the *identity layer* (how buyers recognise and recall you). Most arguments end once each side names its layer. [Sharp] [Neumeier]
2. **Score your situation** on the decision table below.
3. **If difference leads:** narrow to a category or subcategory you can lead, write onliness, and build the launch story on the new category, since newness earns coverage. [Ries & Ries] [Neumeier] Pick distinctive assets on day one anyway, so early attention sticks to your name.
4. **If distinctiveness leads:** stop rewriting the value proposition. Hold your assets, reach every category buyer continuously, and match rivals on table stakes to remove reasons not to buy. [Sharp]
5. **Reconcile targeting.** Choose a narrow *market* you can reach almost all of (a region, use case or buyer type), then speak to every buyer in it, not only the fans. A "niche" is a reachable patch, not a personality tribe inside a mass market; Sharp himself suggests growing new brands one area at a time. [Sharp] [Ries & Ries] [Neumeier]
6. **Use meaning as a generator, not a goal.** Purpose and archetype keep creative consistent; score them by brand-linked recall and penetration. [Mark & Pearson] [Sharp]

## Decision table
| Signal | Difference leads | Distinctiveness leads |
|---|---|---|
| Category in buyers' heads | Unclear, or you are creating one | Known, with familiar rivals |
| Stage | Pre-PMF or launch; you need a reason to try and to be covered | Post-PMF; growth means more buyers |
| Purchase type | Rare, high-stakes, compared side by side (most B2B) | Frequent, low-involvement, habitual |
| Rank | #3 or lower behind a strong leader | Leader or close #2 |
| Copyability | Hard to copy (process, network, data, IP) | A rival can match it within months |
| Price | You need a premium for a comparable offer | You price near the category |
| Decider | An expert or committee reading comparisons | A busy buyer who takes the first good-enough option |

Four or more ticks in one column: lead with that side. If mixed (common), differ at the offer layer, invest in assets at the identity layer, and re-score every six months; the answer drifts toward Sharp as you grow.

## Rules of thumb
- A difference buyers can't notice in seconds doesn't exist for marketing, however real it is.
- Newness earns press and consistency earns memory. Launch with the first, scale with the second. [Ries & Ries] [Sharp]
- Real difference often tests as "weird", but weird isn't proof; it must also be good. Settle it with behaviour, not panels. [Neumeier]
- Our B2B synthesis: memory decides the shortlist and difference wins the comparison, so most B2B startups need both.
- Money test: if buyers won't pay more for your version of an equal offer, spend the next dollar on availability. [Helmer]

## Mistakes
- Citing Sharp for a me-too product, or Zag for ignoring reach. A unique brand nobody remembers loses to a familiar, adequate one.
- Marketing only to superfans, which keeps a brand small. [Sharp]
- Claiming table stakes ("secure", "easy", "fast") as the difference.

## Validate it
- **Assumption:** `A#: buyers in <market> notice and value <difference> [ASSUMPTION, E0]`.
- **Cheapest test:** show 10 category buyers your page and two rivals' pages with names and logos hidden. Ask what each one does differently. Pass if 7 of 10 pick yours out by its difference: **E2**.
- **Money test:** offer the same package at a premium against a parity option. Purchases are **E4**, the only grade that settles whether difference pays.
- **Assumption:** `A#: our assets are recognisable without the name`. Test it with `distinctive-assets-and-mental-availability.md`.
- "We're unique" from the founder or the AI is **E0**.
