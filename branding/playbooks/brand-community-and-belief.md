# Build belief, community and real loyalty (not discounts)
**Use when:** you want advocates or a following, you only win on price, or someone proposes a loyalty or points program.  ·  **Sources:** Primal Branding (Hanlon), Start With Why (Sinek), This Is Marketing (Godin), How Brands Grow (Sharp), Zag (Neumeier)

## Method
1. **Name the community's job honestly.** It could be product insight and co-creation, launch word of mouth, belonging in an identity category, or employer brand. Don't count on it to be the growth engine. Superfans are few, they reach fewer people than you'd hope, and loyalty mostly tracks market share. [Sharp] Community deepens meaning, and reach still drives growth.
2. **Audit the primal code.** Mark each of the seven pieces present, missing or stale. [Hanlon]
   - *Creation story:* where you came from and why.
   - *Creed:* the one belief you want people to hold, short enough for a sign.
   - *Icons:* the sensory cues.
   - *Rituals:* the repeated interactions.
   - *Non-believers:* who and what you are not.
   - *Sacred words:* the vocabulary insiders learn.
   - *Leader:* the person carrying the vision.
3. **Write the creed, and cast the non-believers as a way of working, not as people.** Mocking customers who haven't switched backfires, especially in B2B. [Hanlon]
4. **Draw the ritual timeline.** Run it from first hearing about you, through buying, onboarding, use, support and renewal. Mark each step positive or negative, fix the worst negative, and make one ordinary moment memorable. [Hanlon]
5. **Start with the smallest group who already share the worldview.** Give them a clear marker of what people like them do, and something easy to say about you. [Godin] [Sinek] The mainstream follows only once it sees proof from people like itself, so collect references from pragmatic buyers early. [Moore]
6. **Market with members, not at them.** Give them roles, early access, credit and tools. Let them coin their own words and rituals. Start with employees, the most overlooked believers. [Hanlon]
7. **Replace discounts with mutual loyalty.** The company acts loyal first, through service, privileges, access and recognition. Points-for-discount schemes mostly enrol people who already buy, and they teach everyone to wait for deals. [Neumeier] [Sharp] [Sinek]
8. **Wean off manipulation.** Track the share of sales that depends on price cuts, promotions, fear or novelty. These buy transactions, not loyalty. Lower the share as belief grows. [Sinek]
9. **Never pay for fake belief.** Paid endorsements need disclosure, and endorsers should already hold your belief. [Sinek]

## Rules of thumb
- Without a ritual, a community is just a group chat. Ritual is what brings people back.
- Measure referrals and penetration, not member counts.
- Changing a ritual regulars love costs more than it saves. Ask them first. [Hanlon]
- No fans after real usage usually points to a product problem, not a branding problem.
- Repeat purchase isn't loyalty. Loyalty means staying when a cheaper or better option appears. [Sinek]
- Communities work best where buying signals identity: tools for a craft, causes, hobbies, local brands, developer products.

## Mistakes
- Launching a forum with no creed, ritual or reason to return.
- Treating a points program as brand strategy.
- Expecting the top 1% of fans to deliver half of growth. [Sharp]
- Training customers to expect discounts, then struggling to raise prices.
- An insider culture so closed that newcomers feel unwelcome. [Hanlon]

## Validate it
- **Assumption:** `A#: members refer new customers [ASSUMPTION, E0]`. Test: record the referral source for every new customer for 60 days. Observed referrals are **E2**, and referred customers who pay are **E4**.
- **Assumption:** `A#: customers know our story and creed`. Test: ask five customers and five staff to tell the creation story. The count who can is **E2**. Under half means the code isn't landing.
- **Loyalty program:** before launch, hold out a matched group of non-members and compare purchase data after 90 days. A difference in repeat purchase or category spend is **E4**. A member survey is **E0–E1**.
