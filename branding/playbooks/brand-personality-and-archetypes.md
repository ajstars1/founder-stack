# Choose a brand personality and voice that holds
**Use when:** the voice shifts every campaign, the team argues over adjective lists, or you need a voice guide before hiring writers or designers.  ·  **Sources:** The Hero and the Outlaw (Mark & Pearson), Primal Branding (Hanlon), The Brand Gap (Neumeier), The 22 Immutable Laws of Branding (Ries & Ries), How Brands Grow (Sharp)

## Method
1. **Write the brand's biography.** Cover the origin, the founder's story, the best piece of communication so far, and what customers say about you unprompted. Personality is mostly found here, not invented. [Mark & Pearson]
2. **Find the product truth that can carry meaning.** An archetype has to be believable from what the product actually does. Never graft a meaning onto a function that can't hold it. [Mark & Pearson]
3. **Map competitors' archetypes.** Chart their lasting identities, not one-off campaigns. Look for an open archetype, or a deeper level of a crowded one. [Mark & Pearson]
4. **Name the customer's unmet yearning.** Appeal to what is missing in their life, not a mirror of who they already are. [Mark & Pearson]
5. **Pick one archetype.** Each founder chooses alone, then compare. Use the four drives to narrow the field quickly [Mark & Pearson]:

| Drive | Archetypes | Typical founder fit |
|---|---|---|
| Independence and fulfilment | Innocent, Explorer, Sage | Simplicity, freedom, expertise and research |
| Mastery and risk | Hero, Outlaw, Magician | Performance, challenger, transformation tech |
| Belonging | Regular Guy/Gal, Lover, Jester | Everyday value, intimacy and beauty, fun |
| Stability and control | Caregiver, Creator, Ruler | Care and service, tools for makers, premium and control |

6. **Respect the category's essence.** Every category has a meaning buyers expect, such as safety in childcare or competence in accounting. If a rival owns it, you can pick a different archetype, but don't violate that essence. [Mark & Pearson]
7. **Check that the culture can live it.** Ask whether the team behaves this way when nobody is marketing. A caring brand with a cold support process fails fast. [Mark & Pearson] [Hanlon]
8. **Turn the archetype into a voice guide:** three traits, each with "this, not that"; your own vocabulary (the words insiders learn) [Hanlon]; words you never use; and three sample lines.
9. **Know your archetype's shadow.** A Hero can tip into arrogance, a Sage into lecturing, a Jester into flippancy. Write the guardrail down. [Mark & Pearson]
10. **Fix the archetype, rotate the stories.** Campaigns change, character doesn't. [Mark & Pearson] [Ries & Ries]
11. **Run the hand test.** Cover the logo; can buyers still tell it's you? [Neumeier]

## Sharp's caveat
Buying data suggest that personality ratings mostly track brand size and usage, not choice. [Sharp] Use the archetype as a tool for consistency and alignment, not as a growth engine, and judge it by whether your output becomes recognisable.

## Rules of thumb
- One archetype. Blending three produces mush. [Mark & Pearson]
- A Hero-archetype brand still casts the *customer* as the hero in its copy and acts as guide. [Miller] [Mark & Pearson]
- Each archetype markets in its own way. A Caregiver shows care rather than claiming it. A Sage earns credibility and avoids the hard sell. A Ruler gives the buyer control. [Mark & Pearson]
- Challengers default to Outlaw, but an Outlaw voice sold to compliance officers backfires. Choose by buyer and category, not by founder taste.
- B2B often fits Sage, Creator, Caregiver, Ruler or Hero. A contrasting archetype that respects category essence can stand out.
- Shift to a deeper level of the same archetype when the market matures. Switching archetypes throws away equity. [Mark & Pearson]

## Mistakes
- Adjective soup ("innovative, friendly, trustworthy, bold"). It describes every brand.
- Changing archetype with every agency or campaign.
- Choosing an archetype the product can't back up.
- Being edgy for attention. It wins notice but not identification. [Mark & Pearson]
- A voice guide that lives in a PDF nobody opens. Put the sample lines in writers' templates.

## Template
```
PERSONALITY WORKSHEET: <brand>, <date>
Biography: origin ___ founder story ___ best communication ___ what customers say ___
Product truth that carries meaning:
Competitor map: <rival> = <archetype, level>; ...
Customer's unmet yearning:
Founder picks (blind): A ___  B ___  C ___   → Chosen: ___
Category essence to respect:
Culture check: where we already live it ___ where we don't ___
Shadow to guard against:

VOICE GUIDE
| Trait | This | Not that | Sample line |
|-------|------|----------|-------------|
Our words: ...          Never say: ...
Assumptions logged: A#, A#
```

## Validate it
- **Assumption:** `A#: our voice is recognisable without the logo [ASSUMPTION, E0]`.
- **Cheapest test:** mix five unbranded pieces of yours with five from rivals. Ask 10 target buyers to sort them by brand, or to say which feel alike. Pass if 7 of 10 group yours correctly: **E2**.
- **Fit:** pair the archetype-led version of a page or ad against a neutral one on real traffic. Engagement and conversion are **E2–E3**.
- Founders agreeing on the blind pick shows internal alignment only. Buyers saying they "like the personality" is **E0**.
