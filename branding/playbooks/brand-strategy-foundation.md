# Set the brand foundation: what we stand for, and why it's ours
**Use when:** there is no written brand platform, founders describe the company differently, or every design and copy decision gets reopened.  ·  **Sources:** Start With Why (Sinek), Zag (Neumeier), The 22 Immutable Laws of Branding (Ries & Ries), How Brands Grow (Sharp), The Brand Gap (Neumeier)

## Method
1. **Check that positioning exists.** You need the target customer, the alternative they use today, and the value only you deliver. If any is missing, do `/gtm` first. The foundation sits on top of positioning and cannot replace it. [Dunford]
2. **Run the Focus Test, blind.** Each founder writes one sentence each for who we are, what we do and why it matters. Compare the answers. Where they diverge is your first finding. [Neumeier]
3. **Dig up the purpose; don't invent it.** Look back at the founders' history for the pattern that keeps recurring: why they started, what they refuse to do, which problems make them angry. Write it as a belief about the world, with no product words and no revenue. [Sinek]
4. **Turn values into verbs.** Write 3–5 behaviours you could hold someone to, such as "reply to every customer within one working day". Virtue nouns like "integrity" don't count. [Sinek]
5. **Name the enemy.** It should be the old way of doing the job, not a company. It gives the purpose an edge and gives messaging a villain. [Neumeier] [Miller]
6. **Write the onliness statement.** Fill in: only [category] that [difference] for [who] in [where] who need [need] while [trend]. List every rival who could truthfully sign it, then rewrite until none can. [Neumeier]
7. **Pick the one word.** Choose the single attribute or narrowed category you want to own in buyers' minds. It must be one that no rival already holds. [Ries & Ries]
8. **Draft the trueline.** This is the one true, valuable, believable thing rivals can't or won't claim: a single proposition, with no "and". It stays internal, and taglines are later written from it. [Neumeier]
9. **Apply Sharp's critique before you commit.** (a) Most buyers will never learn your purpose, so treat it as a compass for the team and for creative work, not a reason to buy. (b) Would a busy, light buyer notice your difference within seconds? If not, the brand still needs distinctive assets and reach. See `differentiation-vs-distinctiveness.md`. [Sharp]
10. **Run the Celery Test on your last five decisions.** Mark which ones the purpose would have changed. If it changes none, it is too vague to be useful. [Sinek]

## Rules of thumb
- If the purpose could hang on any company's wall, it fails. Swap in a rival's name; if it still reads true, rewrite it.
- Purpose earns its keep in hiring, choosing extensions, fundraising and community. On a conversion page, lead with the customer's problem (`messaging-and-brandscript.md`).
- Ries's one word and Neumeier's onliness are the same discipline at different zoom. The word is what sits in the buyer's mind. Onliness is the full strategic claim behind it.
- If onliness needs commas or "and", you have two brands or none.
- The foundation fits on one page.
- Revise when positioning changes, not when the team gets bored.

## Mistakes
- A "why" that is really a what ("the best AI for invoices") or a money goal.
- Borrowing a famous brand's purpose. Copied behaviour without the belief behind it doesn't hold up. [Sinek]
- Treating this page as positioning and skipping `/gtm`.
- Expecting the purpose statement to drive purchase. It drives consistency, and reach and memory drive purchase. [Sharp]
- Values written as nouns that nobody can be held to.

## Validate it
- **Assumptions created:** `A#: target buyers notice and value <difference> [ASSUMPTION, E0]`. `A#: the team shares one understanding of the purpose [ASSUMPTION, E0]`.
- **Internal check (not customer evidence):** five team members write the Focus Test blind. Pass if 4 of 5 agree on meaning.
- **Cheapest external test:** say a one-liner built from the trueline to 10 target buyers. Ten minutes later, ask what makes the company different. Pass if 6 of 10 restate the difference unprompted, which is **E2** (observed recall).
- **Stronger:** run two landing pages, trueline-led vs a generic category claim. Booked calls or signups are **E3**. If people pay a premium for an otherwise equal offer, that is **E4**, and only E4 shows the difference is worth money. [Helmer]
- Founder conviction, AI agreement or "love it" from friends stays **E0**.
