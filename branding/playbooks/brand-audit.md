# Audit the brand: a diagnostic across all the disciplines
**Use when:** you need a health check before a raise, rebrand or new hire; after a merger or fast growth; or when nobody can say what the company stands for.  ·  **Sources:** Zag and The Brand Gap (Neumeier), How Brands Grow (Sharp), Building a StoryBrand (Miller), Primal Branding (Hanlon), Start With Why (Sinek)

## Method
1. **Collect the artefacts.** Gather the website, pitch deck, ads, packaging, product UI, onboarding and sales emails, support replies, social posts, job ads and the office or store. Lay them side by side.
2. **Interview three groups, five people each.** Customers, category buyers who aren't customers, and staff. Ask what the company does, who it's for, how it differs, and what they picture when they hear the name. Let them answer unprompted, before you show anything. Auditing only from the inside produces brand boosterism. [Hanlon]
3. **Score eight lenses, 0–2 each**, with the evidence written next to each score:
   1. *Clarity* [Neumeier] [Miller]: Focus Test answers agree, and the homepage passes the five-second grunt test.
   2. *Difference* [Neumeier] [Ries & Ries]: the onliness can't be signed by a rival, and buyers restate the difference unprompted.
   3. *Distinctiveness* [Sharp]: assets score on fame and uniqueness, and the brand survives the swap test and hand test. [Neumeier]
   4. *Availability* [Sharp]: unprompted recall at buying cues, and how easy you are to find and buy.
   5. *Consistency* [Mark & Pearson] [Ries & Ries]: one archetype and voice across artefacts, and assets unchanged across campaigns.
   6. *Inside-out alignment* [Sinek] [Mark & Pearson]: staff state the same purpose, values show up as behaviour, the culture lives the archetype, and it would survive the founder leaving (School Bus Test).
   7. *Belief and experience* [Hanlon] [Neumeier]: the seven pieces of primal code are present, and the ritual timeline has few negatives.
   8. *Scope and dependence* [Ries & Ries] [Sinek] [Sharp]: there are no blurring extensions or muddy-middle architecture, and a low share of sales depends on discounts.
4. **Read the pattern, not just the total:**
   - Low clarity with high consistency: you are consistently confusing. Fix messaging first.
   - High difference with low distinctiveness: buyers like the idea and forget the name. Fix assets and reach.
   - High distinctiveness with low difference: buyers recognise you but see no reason to pick you. Go back to `/gtm`.
   - Low inside-out alignment with high external scores: the brand rests on the founder, so write it down before you scale.
5. **Pick the two weakest lenses that limit growth** and route each to its playbook. Fix two things, not eight.
6. **Re-audit in six months** with the same questions and people of the same type.

## Rules of thumb
- Customer and buyer words beat internal opinion every time. Quote what they said; don't summarise it into what you hoped.
- Non-customers matter most. Customers already know you, which inflates every score. [Sharp]
- A lens scored 2 without evidence is a 0.
- At seed stage, clarity, difference and availability matter most. Belief and architecture matter more later.
- The audit is diagnostic. Positioning faults go to `/gtm`, and execution faults go to the matching playbook here.

## Mistakes
- Auditing the logo and calling it a brand audit.
- Interviewing only happy customers.
- Fixing all eight lenses at once and finishing none.
- Treating staff enthusiasm as evidence that customers feel the same. [Hanlon]
- Skipping the baseline, so the next audit can't show change.

## Validate it
- Every score carries its own grade. Interview answers about past behaviour ("I chose you because a colleague used you") are **E1**. Five-second tests, recall tests and asset recognition are **E2**. Referrals, booked calls and renewals pulled from your own data are **E2–E4**, depending on whether money moved.
- **Assumption created:** `A#: fixing <lens> will lift <metric> [ASSUMPTION, E0]`. Log it with the metric and target, then let `/experiments` design the before/after test.
- An audit built only on internal opinion is **E0** throughout. Say so in the report.
