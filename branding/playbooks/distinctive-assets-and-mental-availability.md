# Get remembered: distinctive assets, mental and physical availability
**Use when:** nobody recalls the brand, ads get credited to rivals, the team is choosing logo, colour, mascot or sound, or a brand is launching from zero awareness.  ·  **Sources:** How Brands Grow (Sharp), The 22 Immutable Laws of Branding (Ries & Ries), Primal Branding (Hanlon), The Brand Gap (Neumeier)

## Method
1. **List candidate assets.** These are anything besides the name that can signal the brand: a colour, a mark or shape, a wordmark style, a character, a tagline, a sound, a packaging form, an ad format, or a recurring person. Any of the five senses can carry one. [Sharp] [Hanlon]
2. **Map the category first.** Chart rivals' colours, shapes, type and imagery. If the leader owns a colour, take a clearly different one, and avoid category clichés like the blue shield in security or the green leaf in wellness. [Ries & Ries]
3. **Choose few, and commit.** One colour, one mark, one type voice, and at most one character or sound. A few strong assets beat many weak ones.
4. **Always pair assets with the name at first.** Buyers learn an asset by seeing it next to the brand name, repeatedly, for years. Only drop the name once research shows the asset works alone. [Sharp]
5. **Measure fame and uniqueness.** Fame is the share of category buyers who link the asset to you. Uniqueness is the share who link it only to you. [Sharp] Act on the grid:
   - High fame, high uniqueness: use it alone, and protect it.
   - Low fame, high uniqueness: invest. Show it everywhere with the name.
   - High fame, low uniqueness: it's shared with rivals. Always pair it with the name.
   - Low on both: drop it or rebuild it.
6. **Map buying cues.** List the moments, needs and places when buyers think of the category, in their own words, such as "month-end invoicing" or "my landlord just raised rent". Link the brand to those cues in every touchpoint. Your real competitors are whoever else is linked to the same cue. [Sharp]
7. **Fix physical availability.** Make the brand easy to find and buy: search results, app stores, marketplaces, distributors, payment options, checkout steps, opening hours. [Sharp]
8. **For a new brand, teach four things together:** what it does, what it looks like, what it's called, and where to get it. [Sharp]
9. **Run the swap test and the hand test.** Put a rival's mark on your materials. If nothing is lost, neither mark is distinctive. Then cover your logo: if nobody can tell whose piece it is, the voice and look are generic. [Neumeier]
10. **Brand every piece early and clearly.** Make the name or a key asset visible in the first seconds of a video and in the headline area of an ad. A memorable ad that buyers can't attribute to you is wasted money. [Sharp]

## Rules of thumb
- Keep the assets, and refresh the stories, layouts and campaigns around them. [Sharp] [Ries & Ries]
- A logo must be legible first and clever second. Horizontal marks fit most placements. [Ries & Ries]
- Use one colour everywhere, including slides, invoices, the product UI and the office.
- Continuous presence beats bursts. Being forgotten between campaigns resets your progress. [Sharp]
- Measure with category buyers, not customers: your users recognise everything about you, which inflates the scores. [Sharp]
- Physical spaces and products can carry sound, touch or smell assets, which few competitors bother to own. [Hanlon]

## Mistakes
- Redesigning because the team is bored. Buyers have barely started to learn the old assets.
- Copying the category leader's colour to look established.
- Rotating three palettes and five illustration styles across channels.
- Reading follower counts as mental availability. They measure fans, not memory among buyers.
- Spending the brand budget on design while the product is hard to find or buy.

## Validate it
- **Assumption:** `A#: category buyers link <asset> to <brand> [ASSUMPTION, E0]`.
- **Cheapest test:** show the asset with the brand name removed to 20 or more category buyers who aren't customers, and ask which brand it belongs to. That gives fame and uniqueness percentages: **E2** (observed recognition). Fewer than 20 people gives direction, not a verdict.
- **Mental availability:** ask "Which brands come to mind when <cue>?" with no prompting, and track the share naming you every quarter: **E2**.
- **Outcome:** a rise in the share of category buyers who bought in the period (penetration) or in branded search, measured in sales data, is **E4**. Assets are working when this moves while price stays flat.
- An internal vote on the logo is **E0**, however senior the voters.
