# Decide brand architecture: same name, sub-brand or a new brand?
**Use when:** a new product, tier, market or acquisition is coming and someone asks whether it should carry the main name.  ·  **Sources:** The 22 Immutable Laws of Branding (Ries & Ries), Zag (Neumeier), The Hero and the Outlaw (Mark & Pearson), Start With Why (Sinek), How Brands Grow (Sharp)

## Method
1. **State what the master brand means in buyers' minds, with evidence.** Ask 10 buyers unprompted what the brand is and does. Use their answer, not the founder's wish. [Ries & Ries] [Mark & Pearson]
2. **Classify the new offer:**
   - (a) a variant in the same category for the same buyer
   - (b) an adjacent category for the same buyer
   - (c) a new category
   - (d) a lower price tier
   - (e) a higher price tier
   - (f) a different buyer
3. **Run five filters:**
   - *Focus* [Ries & Ries]: would the name on this blur the one word you own? If the market is shifting to a new category, launch a second brand rather than an extension.
   - *Purpose* (Celery Test) [Sinek]: does the offer prove what you believe, or contradict it?
   - *Brand Bank* [Mark & Pearson]: is the meaning strong enough to spend? Every extension is a withdrawal. Name the deposit that offsets it.
   - *Four dangers* [Neumeier]: contagion (a failure here hurts the core), confusion (buyers can't tell what you are), contradiction (it clashes with the meaning in some markets), complexity (too many variants to manage).
   - *Price direction* [Neumeier] [Ries & Ries] [Sinek]: stretching down drags the core's value down. Stretching up under the same name rarely convinces. Premium and budget moves usually need their own name.
4. **Choose the model** [Neumeier]:
   - *Branded house:* one name, with descriptive product names. It's cheap, builds one asset, and suits startups.
   - *House of brands:* separate names, used when buyers, price tiers or risks differ.
   - *Endorsed:* a separate name "by <Master>". Use it only with a written rule for when it applies.
   - Avoid the muddy middle: a mix of half-brands with no rule.
5. **Model cannibalisation realistically.** Two brands in your portfolio will share buyers roughly in proportion to their size, like any rivals. Similar brands can coexist, but expect overlap. [Sharp]
6. **Write the rule.** For example: "New products are descriptive names under the master brand unless filter X, Y or Z fails." Future decisions then become quick and consistent.

## Startup default
Run one brand with plain product names until you lead your first category. Leaders can bend the rules for a while, but challengers can't. [Ries & Ries] Use a separate brand only when the new offer contradicts the core meaning, serves a different buyer, sits at a very different price, or carries a risk that could spill onto the core.

## Rules of thumb
- Buyers want choice between brands, not a maze within one. [Neumeier]
- Sub-brands are mostly an inside-out idea. Buyers see the master brand and a model name. [Ries & Ries]
- Judge an extension by what happens to the core, not just by the extension's own sales. [Ries & Ries]
- "Why should we limit ourselves?" is the warning sentence. [Ries & Ries]
- A good extension sharpens what the brand stands for, and a bad one trades the long term for this quarter's revenue. [Neumeier]
- If buyers already name your *meaning* unprompted, you can stretch further than a brand that stands for a product category. [Mark & Pearson] [Sinek]

## Mistakes
- The doom loop: extend for growth, blur the brand, lose growth, extend again. [Neumeier]
- Giving every feature its own name and logo.
- Using a famous master brand to prop up a weak product.
- Creating a new brand for every small launch, multiplying cost and splitting attention.
- Merging two brands by org politics instead of by which one buyers remember better.

## Validate it
- **Assumption:** `A#: buyers accept <master brand> in <new category> [ASSUMPTION, E0]`.
- **Cheapest test:** two fake-door landing pages with identical copy, one under the master brand and one under a new name, on the same paid traffic. Waitlist signups are **E3**, and pre-orders or deposits are **E4**.
- **After launch:** watch the core's conversion, branded search and unprompted association each month. Erosion seen in those numbers is **E2**, and it outranks the extension's revenue as the verdict.
