# Fix a confusing message: BrandScript, one-liner, homepage wireframe
**Use when:** prospects can't say what you do, the homepage doesn't convert, each team member pitches differently, or you need a one-liner.  ·  **Sources:** Building a StoryBrand (Miller), Made to Stick (Heath & Heath), Start With Why (Sinek), The Brand Gap (Neumeier)

## Method
1. **Start from positioning.** Messaging puts a chosen position into words; it can't pick the position. Pull the target customer, the alternative and the unique value from `/gtm`. [Miller] [Dunford]
2. **Fill the BrandScript, one choice per box** [Miller]:
   - *Character:* the customer, and the one thing they want, tied to a basic drive: save time or money, gain status, belong, grow revenue, cut waste.
   - *Problem:* a villain (a root cause, preferably a way of working rather than a company), the external problem, the internal frustration it causes, and why it's simply wrong.
   - *Guide:* a line of empathy ("we know what it's like to ...") plus up to three proofs: numbers, short testimonials, logos.
   - *Plan:* three steps to buy or start, plus an agreement plan: promises that answer the buyer's fears.
   - *Calls to action:* a direct one (buy, book) and a transitional one (a free guide, sample or trial).
   - *Failure:* two or three stakes of doing nothing. Use them sparingly, like salt.
   - *Success:* specific outcomes, and who the customer becomes ("from ___ to ___").
3. **Find the core.** If the buyer remembers only one sentence, which should it be? Strip the jargon you no longer notice, and make the claim concrete with a number, an example or an image. [Heath]
4. **Write the one-liner** from the problem, your plan and the resulting success, in under 25 words. Test it aloud until listeners ask a follow-up question instead of nodding politely. [Miller]
5. **Place the purpose correctly.** On conversion pages, lead with the customer's problem and cast them as the hero. In recruiting, fundraising and community, open with the belief, phrased as something the customer shares. [Miller] [Sinek]
6. **Wireframe the homepage from the BrandScript** [Miller] [Neumeier]:
   1. Above the fold: what you offer in plain words, who it's for, the direct CTA, and the transitional CTA.
   2. Stakes: the problem and its cost.
   3. Guide: empathy plus proof.
   4. Plan: three steps.
   5. Success: what life looks like after.
   6. Offer breakdown or pricing, then a short explanatory paragraph for search and skimmers.
   7. Repeat the direct CTA.
   It follows the visitor's reading order: notice, what is it, why care, persuade me, prove it.
7. **Run the grunt test.** In five seconds, can a stranger say what you offer, how it improves their life, and how to get it? [Miller]
8. **Hand off.** Headlines, ad variants, email sequences and offer construction go to `/marketing`. Sales scripts go to `/sales`.

## Rules of thumb
- Customers buy what they understand fastest, not what's objectively best. [Miller]
- Pick one desire. Every extra desire weakens the pull. [Miller]
- Sell to the internal problem, and prove you solve the external one. [Miller]
- Talking about yourself makes you the hero, which puts you in competition with your own buyer. [Miller]
- Fewer words get read. Most first drafts can lose half their words. [Miller] [Heath]
- Every team member should say the same one-liner. Test it by asking them.

## Mistakes
- Opening with company history, awards or "passionate innovators since 2019".
- Stacking several value propositions in one headline.
- Offering only "Learn more" with no direct CTA, or only "Buy now" with no low-risk step.
- Turning fear up so high that it repels buyers or feels manipulative.
- Writing copy before positioning, then blaming the copy.

## Template
```
BRANDSCRIPT: <offer>, <date>
Character wants (one thing):
Villain:          External problem:
Internal problem: Philosophical (why it's wrong):
Guide, empathy line:            Proof (≤3):
Plan: 1. ___  2. ___  3. ___    Agreement promises:
Direct CTA:                     Transitional CTA:
Stakes (2–3):
Success outcomes:               From ___ → To ___

ONE-LINER (<25 words): <problem> ... <plan> ... <success>

HOMEPAGE WIREFRAME
[Hero] Offer: ___ | For: ___ | [Direct CTA] [Transitional CTA]
[Stakes] ___
[Guide] Empathy: ___  Proof: ___
[Plan] 1/2/3
[Success] ___
[Offer / pricing] ___   [Explanatory paragraph] ___
[Repeat direct CTA]
Assumptions logged: A#, A#
```

## Validate it
- **Assumption:** `A#: visitors understand the offer within 5 seconds [ASSUMPTION, E0]`.
- **Cheapest test:** a five-second test with 5–10 target buyers. Show the hero section, hide it, and ask what it offers and who it's for. Pass if 4 of 5 answer correctly: **E2**.
- **One-liner:** say it to 10 target buyers at events or on calls. Count how many ask for a demo, an intro or a next step. That counts as **E3** once a meeting is scheduled.
- **Conversion:** A/B the new hero against the old on real traffic. Booked calls or signups are **E3**, and paid conversions are **E4**. Run until each arm has a meaningful count; `/experiments` sets the stopping rule.
- Teammates saying "much clearer" is **E0**.
