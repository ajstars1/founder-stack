# Decide whether to rebrand or rename
**Use when:** someone proposes a new name, logo or identity, the current name needs constant explaining, a pivot or merger has happened, or an investor or new hire says the brand "feels off".  ·  **Sources:** The 22 Immutable Laws of Branding (Ries & Ries), How Brands Grow (Sharp), Hello, My Name Is Awesome (Watkins), Primal Branding (Hanlon), Zag (Neumeier)

## Method
1. **State the trigger precisely**, and sort it into one of three tiers:
   - *Must change:* legal conflict, an offensive meaning, a name that blocks sales.
   - *Strategic:* a pivot to a new category, buyer or price tier, or a merger.
   - *Weak:* boredom, a new CMO or an investor's taste.
   Weak triggers justify a refresh at most.
2. **Find out what exists in buyers' minds.** Measure awareness, what buyers associate with you, and which assets they recognise (fame and uniqueness). The change has to happen in their heads, not in a board deck. [Ries & Ries] [Sharp]
3. **Apply Ries's change test.** Change is safest when the brand is weak or absent in minds, when you are moving permanently down market, or when the field moves slowly enough for people to forget. If you hold a clear perception, changing it is a gamble. [Ries & Ries]
4. **Climb the change ladder only as far as the trigger needs:**
   1. Refresh: same assets, updated execution.
   2. Evolve: keep the recognised assets, fix the weak ones.
   3. Rename: new name, carry the assets across.
   4. Full rebrand: new name and new identity.
   5. Second brand: launch a new brand while the old one funds it, for a dying core market. [Neumeier]
5. **Weigh a rename** [Watkins]. Pros: you stop explaining the name, get a fresh start and a reason to contact every customer, and win over future customers who will only know the new name. Cons: cost, attachment, search and listing churn, legal paperwork and hurt feelings. Startup heuristic: if most of your future customers haven't heard of you yet, rename now. It only gets more expensive.
6. **Carry over what works.** Keep any asset with real fame and uniqueness unless it *is* the problem. [Sharp]
7. **Launch inside first.** Retell the creation story with the change as a new chapter, and confirm the creed and rituals. In a merger, decide which rituals carry over, or staff will feel the culture vanish. [Hanlon]
8. **Launch outside with a reason.** Tell the story of why you changed, run a "formerly X" period, set up redirects, update listings, handles and legal records, and tell key accounts personally.
9. **Measure before and after** against a baseline taken in step 2.

## Rules of thumb
- Boredom is not a reason. Buyers notice your assets far less than your team does. [Sharp]
- Pre-PMF with few customers: rename freely. With real recognition: evolve, don't replace.
- Legal or offensive problems: change fast and completely. Half-measures cost twice.
- In a merger, keep the brand that buyers remember better, measured, not the one whose team won the negotiation. [Ries & Ries]
- A pivot to a new category for a new buyer usually earns a new name. A pivot within the category usually doesn't. [Ries & Ries]
- A new logo doesn't fix a strategy problem. Fix the strategy first (`brand-strategy-foundation.md`).

## Mistakes
- Throwing away recognised colours or marks along with the problem.
- Rebranding during a crisis to hide it. Buyers and press notice.
- Announcing a new identity with no story behind it.
- Skipping the internal launch, so staff keep using the old name for months.
- Treating a domain purchase as trademark clearance. [Watkins]

## Validate it
- **Assumption:** `A#: the current name or brand is costing us deals [ASSUMPTION, E0]`. Test: for two weeks, log every time you spell or explain the name, misspelled branded searches, and lost deals that cite the brand. These are **E2** counts. "I just don't like it" is **E0**.
- **Assumption:** `A#: the new name performs better`. Run the recall and spelling test in `naming.md` before deciding: **E2**.
- **After the change:** compare branded search, direct traffic, conversion and unprompted recall with the baseline at 30, 90 and 180 days. Conversion and revenue held or improved is **E4**.
