# Branding
Make a young company easy to notice, remember, explain and believe in. Grounded in nine branding books, reconciled into decisions; positioning itself belongs to `/gtm`.

## Start here
1. Read the venture context if it exists: `discovery/00-CONTEXT.md` and `discovery/LEDGER.md`.
   Else ask at most 3 questions (what/for whom, stage + evidence so far, the decision at hand).
2. Route with the table below, read only the matching playbook(s). If the founder cannot say who
   the product is for, what buyers use instead today, and why they would switch, send them to
   `/gtm` first. Branding expresses a position; it does not choose one.
   If two rows fit, pick the one matching the buyer's or user's latest *action*, not their words. Name other relevant playbooks; don't read them.

## Principles
1. A brand is the impression and memory links in buyers' heads; the company can shape it but never owns it. [Neumeier] [Sharp]
2. Clarity beats cleverness: one idea per brand, one proposition per line. [Miller] [Ries & Ries] [Neumeier]
3. Be different in the offer and unmistakable in the branding; these are two layers, not rivals. [Sharp] [Neumeier] [Ries & Ries]
4. Most growth comes from more buyers, mostly light ones. A plan that relies on superfans or points schemes will fall short. [Sharp]
5. Hold your assets and voice for years and refresh only the execution. The team tires of them long before buyers notice them. [Sharp] [Ries & Ries] [Hanlon]
6. Narrow scope strengthens a brand, and every extension spends some of its meaning. [Ries & Ries] [Neumeier] [Mark & Pearson]
7. Build it inside first: if the team cannot state the belief, customers never will. [Sinek] [Hanlon] [Mark & Pearson]
8. In outward copy the customer is the hero; the brand is the guide with empathy and proof. [Miller]
9. Discounts and points rent sales; they do not build preference. [Sharp] [Sinek] [Neumeier]
10. Opinion research misleads here: groups pick the safest name and punish real difference. Test with behaviour. [Watkins] [Neumeier] [Sharp]

Evidence weight: Sharp is the only author working from large buying datasets, though only on established
repeat-purchase brands. Treat his laws as the default, and the other frameworks as creative methods to test against them.

## When the experts disagree
| Tension | Go with A when… | Go with B when… |
|---|---|---|
| A: differentiate [Neumeier] [Ries & Ries] [Sinek] vs B: be distinctive [Sharp] | No category frame exists yet, you're pre-PMF or ranked #3 or lower, the purchase is rare and compared, or you need a premium | Buyers already know the category, purchases are frequent and habitual, you're the leader, or rivals can copy the feature fast |
| A: target the believers [Sinek] [Neumeier] vs B: reach every category buyer [Sharp] | Launching: pick a narrow patch you can actually reach | Scaling: within the chosen patch, reach every buyer, not just the fans |
| A: lead with meaning (purpose, archetype, code) [Mark & Pearson] [Hanlon] vs B: build mental and physical availability [Sharp] | Identity and badge categories, communities, employer brand, nonprofits | Whenever the growth plan depends on zealots; judge meaning by brand-linked recall |
| A: stay focused, launch a second brand [Ries & Ries] vs B: stretch on meaning [Mark & Pearson] [Sinek] | The brand stands for a category word, or you don't yet lead your first category | Customers name the meaning unprompted and the new offer carries it |
| A: customer is hero [Miller] vs B: start with the company's why [Sinek] | Conversion pages, ads, sales emails | Hiring, fundraising, community, extension filters; phrase the why as a belief the customer shares |

## Output contract
Every answer ends with:
- **Recommendation**: the call, specific to their business.
- **Deliverable**: the artifact (statement, shortlist, sequence, script, plan), filled in.
- **Why**: the framework(s) behind it, credited [Author].
- **Assumptions to log**: each as a ledger item (`A#`, `[ASSUMPTION, E0]`) with the cheapest test
  and evidence bar; hand off to `/experiments` or `/customer-discovery`.
  Grades: E0 opinion · E1 told story of past behaviour · E2 observed behaviour/artifact · E3 commitment (time, intro, next step) · E4 money.
- **Next action**: one thing this week.

## Research
Web-search only for current, external facts: competitors' names, colours and taglines, trademark
registers, domain and handle availability, slang and foreign-language meanings, platform rules. Cite
URL + date as `[FACT — url]`. Never web-search for "what the books say"; the playbooks are the method.
No source → `[UNKNOWN]` + research step. Trademark clearance is a lawyer's call; say so.
