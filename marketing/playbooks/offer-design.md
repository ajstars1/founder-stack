# Build an offer buyers can't compare on price
**Use when:** prospects line you up against cheaper look-alikes, close rates are low, or your next idea is a discount.  ·  **Sources:** $100M Offers (Hormozi), Alchemy (Sutherland), Influence (Cialdini), This Is Marketing (Godin)

## Method
1. **Check the market first** [Hormozi]. Score the segment 1–5 on pain, ability to pay, reachability (lists, groups, associations) and growth. Any score of 2 or below: narrow the segment before touching the offer (`audience-and-permission`). No offer rescues a market that can't or won't buy.
2. **Name the dream outcome in the buyer's words** [Hormozi] [Godin]: the destination, not the vehicle, including how others will see them afterwards. Status is usually the real prize.
3. **Score the value equation** [Hormozi]. Value rises with outcome size and the buyer's belief they'll get it, and falls with time to result and effort required. Note the weakest driver. The cheapest gains are usually a faster first win and less work for the buyer.
4. **List every obstacle in sequence** [Hormozi], before, during and after use; aim for 20–30. Tag each with the driver it hurts: "won't work for me" (likelihood), "too slow" (time), "too hard" (effort), "not worth it" (outcome).
5. **Turn each obstacle into a solution with a vehicle** [Hormozi]. Vehicles vary by who does the work (you, together, them), group size (one-to-one, small group, one-to-many), format (live or recorded) and response speed.
6. **Trim and stack** [Hormozi]. Cut costly, low-value items. Keep cheap, high-value ones (tools, templates, checklists), favouring things built once and reused. Bundle the rest, each part named for its benefit.
7. **Add only true enhancers** [Hormozi] [Cialdini]:
   - *Scarcity*: real capacity caps (total, per week, per cohort). Publishing actual capacity is the honest form.
   - *Urgency*: cohort start dates or seasonal promotions with real end dates. Deadlines go on the bonus or price, never on access to help.
   - *Bonuses*: each aimed at a named obstacle or the next problem. Tools beat more training.
   - *Guarantee*: "Get X by Y, or we Z". Unconditional refunds suit low-ticket consumer sales; high-ticket or B2B needs conditions tied to the actions that predict success. Anti-guarantees fit sunk delivery costs with a real reason; performance pay fits measurable outcomes.
8. **Name the offer, not the company** [Hormozi]. Combine three to five of: a reason for now, who it's for, the goal, a time frame, a container word (sprint, audit, challenge). Company and product names belong to `/branding`.
9. **Check the price signal** [Sutherland] [Godin]. The price must match the claimed value; a cheap-looking price says "weak". The level itself comes from `/gtm`.

## Rules of thumb
- Before cutting price, raise confidence (proof, guarantee) or speed up the first result. Both cost less than a discount.
- One unsolved obstacle can lose the sale. On a no, find it and add a bonus that removes it; don't drop the price.
- Against free alternatives, compete on speed.
- Judge a guarantee by the net sales you keep, not the refund rate. A stronger guarantee that lifts sales 30% while doubling a 5% refund rate still nets more. Run the numbers for your own case.
- When response fades, change creative, then headline and name, then the enhancer. Change the core offer and money model last.
- Scarcity pulls hardest when new or contested, and lasts only if real [Cialdini].

## Mistakes
- Polishing the copy when the market is the problem.
- Inflated "worth $X" tags, invented dropouts and countdowns that reset. These cost credibility and can be illegal (see `ethical-persuasion`).
- A broad unconditional guarantee on expensive delivery, or any guarantee wrapped around a weak product. Refunds pile up.
- Discounting the core offer. Discount a small piece, or add a bonus instead.
- Promising bespoke work you'll never systematise. Over-deliver early, then build the repeatable version.
- Repeating case-study multipliers. Some published arithmetic is wrong; recompute every figure from its parts.

## Validate it
- **Assumptions created:** `A#: [segment] wants [outcome] badly enough to buy this offer at the /gtm price [ASSUMPTION, E0]`, and `A#: guarantee G raises net kept sales [ASSUMPTION, E0]`.
- **Cheapest test:** a real, refusable proposal to 10–20 qualified prospects, by sales call or a deposit page. Run two names or guarantees on the same traffic.
- **Evidence bar:** only deposits or payments (E4) validate offer and price. Booked calls or signed pilots (E3, at least 3 independent prospects) validate the message, not the money. "Sounds great" is E0. Test card in `/experiments`; result to `/facts-assumptions-unknowns`.
