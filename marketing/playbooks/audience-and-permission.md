# Pick the smallest audience you can win, and earn the right to keep talking
**Use when:** you're "for everyone" and nobody cares, you must choose a first segment, leads are plentiful but customers are poor, or you want an audience you own.  ·  **Sources:** This Is Marketing (Godin), $100M Series: Lost Chapters (Hormozi), $100M Offers (Hormozi), Contagious (Berger), Made to Stick (Heath & Heath)

## Method
1. **Name the change you want to make** [Godin]. Be specific about how the people you serve will be different afterwards. Avoid two traps: a change so grand nobody can act on it, and a buzzword reverse-engineered from what you already sell.
2. **Define the smallest viable audience** [Godin]. Work out the fewest people whose change would make the work worth doing.
   - Describe them by worldview (what they believe, want and fear), not by age or income.
   - You should be able to spot one in a room.
   - Start at the extreme corner: the people who are impatient for exactly this.
3. **If you already have customers, mine the best ones** [Hormozi].
   - Rank customers by how much you liked working with them, how much they spent and how long they stayed, and keep the top fifth.
   - Find the three to five qualifiers they share.
   - Write those qualifiers into your ads and intake, and stop selling to people who don't fit.
   - The same work becomes worth more because of who the clients are.
4. **Check that the segment is reachable and hungry** [Hormozi]. Name where these people gather (lists, associations, communities, events). If you can't, the segment isn't targetable yet. Also confirm pain, ability to pay and growth (`offer-design`, step 1).
5. **Write the promise** [Godin]: for people who believe ___ and want ___, this helps them get ___. Then be willing to tell everyone else it isn't for them.
6. **Use status and belonging** [Godin] [Berger] [Heath].
   - Ask whether buying moves your buyer toward belonging (who is with me) or toward rank (who is ahead). The customer decides which matters, not you.
   - Make your product the normal choice inside one tight group: what do people like us do?
   - Build an exclusive group (insiders who choose to join) rather than an elite one (ranked from outside).
7. **Create tension, not fear** [Godin]. Name the pattern you're interrupting and offer a way forward. Fear freezes people; tension moves them.
8. **Earn and own permission** [Godin]. Send messages people expect, that feel personal and that are relevant to them.
   - Own the channel (email, SMS, your community) instead of renting reach on a platform's algorithm.
   - Show up on a steady schedule. Trust builds through repetition.
   - The test of real permission: people would notice and ask if you stopped.
9. **Lead the group** [Godin]. Tell your own story (why you), the group's story (why we're alike) and the story of now (why act today).

## Rules of thumb
- Under modest revenue, narrowing usually raises both price and response for the same product [Hormozi].
- A segment chosen by worldview outperforms one chosen by demographics.
- A price that looks cheap reads as nervous. Match the price to the extreme you claim.
- Find your superusers and give them a megaphone. Narrow, specific content spreads further because it brings a particular friend to mind [Berger].
- Hold the goal steady, change strategy rarely, and change tactics freely.
- Where to stop narrowing: in mass consumer categories, penetration evidence argues against tight targeting [Sharp]. Take that tension to `/branding` and `/gtm`. A smallest viable audience fits new behaviours, services, creators and niche B2B best.

## Mistakes
- "Our customer is anyone who…"
- Personas built only from demographics.
- An audience that lives entirely on one rented platform.
- Hype, spam and pressure tactics. Each one spends the permission you've earned.
- Changing the message every week, so nobody hears it enough to trust it.
- Picking a segment you can describe but can't find.

## Validate it
- **Assumption created:** `A#: [segment by worldview] has the pain, the money and a reachable gathering place [ASSUMPTION, E0]`.
- **Cheapest test:**
  1. Run five to ten `/customer-discovery` interviews to get past-behaviour stories (E1).
  2. Find ten real people in the place you named and start a conversation. They exist and you can reach them (E2).
  3. Make them the offer (E3 or E4).
- **Evidence bar:**
  - List growth, open rates and replies are E2.
  - Booked next steps are E3.
  - Paid conversion from the list is E4.
  - "People like this idea" is E0.
  - Log each item in `/facts-assumptions-unknowns`.
