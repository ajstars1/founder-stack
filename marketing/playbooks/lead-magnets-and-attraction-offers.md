# Get the first yes: lead magnets and attraction offers
**Use when:** you need leads or first customers, a free offer is pulling in the wrong people, or no-shows are eating your calendar.  ·  **Sources:** $100M Series: Lost Chapters (Hormozi), Influence (Cialdini), Traction (Weinberg & Mares), This Is Marketing (Godin)

## Method
1. **Choose the job of the front end** [Hormozi]. There are three options:
   - *Volume*: something free.
   - *Qualified volume*: a deep discount on a small piece, or a cheap paid first step.
   - *Premium*: sell the core offer straight.
   New, unproven sales process: wrap the core offer in a free or cheap first step. Go premium once you know how you close.
2. **Pick a format that fits the ticket size and how much the buyer already knows** [Hormozi] [Weinberg]:
   - *A tool, calculator, template, checklist or audit* that fully solves one narrow slice. Free tools on their own pages keep collecting leads for years. Weinberg calls this engineering as marketing.
   - *An education event* (workshop, multi-day challenge, webinar, strategy call) for expensive or complex offers aimed at cold audiences. The bigger the eventual ask, the more teaching has to come first.
   - *A discounted first step* (paid diagnostic, trial session) priced on a slice of the work, never on the core offer.
   - *Free plus a paid add-on* (free A, paid B that the buyer genuinely needs alongside A), or pick-your-price where trust is low.
   - *Freemium*, only for funded software where a free user costs almost nothing to serve. Route the decision to `/gtm` (PLG).
3. **Make it a real gift** [Cialdini] [Godin]. An unrequested, useful, personal gift creates goodwill and a wish to reciprocate. A gift that turns out to be a trap kills that wish and the trust with it. Solve one problem completely, and let the solution reveal the next problem, the one your paid offer solves.
4. **Always say why it's free or cheap** [Hormozi] [Cialdini]. Use a launch, a new location, or a need for case studies. A stated reason raises take-up, so make sure yours is true.
5. **Tune quality with friction** [Hormozi]. You have five dials:
   - qualifying questions
   - how much information you ask for
   - how many steps there are
   - required viewing before booking
   - ad length
   Add friction until junk and no-shows fall, then stop before good buyers start dropping out. Judge by qualified buyers, not lead count.
6. **Turn the lead into permission** [Godin]. Say what you'll send and how often, then send only that. Collecting an email and going silent wastes the asset.
7. **Connect it to the next purchase**: which offer, and when (`money-model`). A magnet with no next step is a cost centre.

## Rules of thumb
- Free beats a tiny price on volume by a wide margin. When a free offer fails anyway, there are three possible reasons: people didn't want it, didn't believe it, or never saw it. Find out which before you change the format.
- Narrow beats broad. A magnet built for one role in one situation outperforms a generic guide, and it pre-selects the buyers you want.
- Asking for a small payment or a card on file raises show-up rates sharply.
- Raise friction as price and complexity rise. Cheap offers want few steps; expensive ones can carry more.
- If lead cost is fine but buyers are poor, change the magnet's topic and the qualifiers in your ad before you touch targeting.
- Count staff time in a "free" lead's cost.

## Mistakes
- An "ultimate guide" that attracts browsers and students instead of buyers.
- Giving away or discounting the core offer, which teaches buyers to wait for the deal.
- Reporting leads, not qualified buyers.
- No follow-up sequence, so the permission you earned is wasted.
- Freemium without the money to carry free users.
- A magnet so complete that nothing is left for the paid offer.

## Template
```
Buyer: ______ in this situation: ______
Narrow problem the magnet solves completely: ______
Format: [tool | calculator | checklist | audit | workshop | challenge | paid diagnostic | trial | free+add-on]
Reason it's free or cheap (true): ______
The next problem it reveals, which the paid offer solves: ______
Friction dials:  qualifiers __ · fields __ · steps __ · pre-booking content __ · ad length __
Promise of what comes next: "You'll get ______ every ______"
Next offer and moment: ______ at ______
Metrics: opt-in % __ · show/book % __ · buyers per 100 leads __ · cost per qualified buyer __
```

## Validate it
- **Assumption created:** `A#: [magnet] attracts [segment], and at least X% of opt-ins buy [next offer] within N days [ASSUMPTION, E0]`.
- **Cheapest test:** build a minimum version (a shared doc, a spreadsheet tool, or one live workshop) and push it through one channel for a week. Set your pass bar before launch.
- **Evidence bar:**
  - Opt-ins are E2: observed behaviour, but cheap.
  - Booked calls or attendance are E3. A working pass needs E3 from at least 3 independent sources.
  - Purchases are E4, and only E4 validates the downstream conversion rate and any price.
  - Ask in every booked call where they found you and what nearly stopped them. Those answers are E1 and guide the next version.
  - Record the test in `/experiments`.
