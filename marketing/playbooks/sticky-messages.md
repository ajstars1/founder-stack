# Make the message understood, remembered and acted on
**Use when:** a pitch, homepage, mission or strategy gets nods but changes nothing, or experts are explaining the product to novices.  ·  **Sources:** Made to Stick (Heath & Heath), Contagious (Berger), Breakthrough Advertising (Schwartz), This Is Marketing (Godin)

## Method
1. **Find the core** [Heath]. Write the one sentence that would still guide decisions if everything else were forgotten: "If they remember one thing, it is ___." For internal plans, use the commander's-intent form: "Whatever else happens, we must ___."
2. **Assume the curse of knowledge** [Heath]. You can't un-know what you know, so asking yourself "will they get it?" tells you nothing. Test the message on someone outside the field.
3. **Score the draft on SUCCESs** [Heath] (Simple, Unexpected, Concrete, Credible, Emotional, Stories). Give each a score from 0 to 3 and fix the two weakest. You don't need all six.
   - *Simple*: the core, said compactly. Borrow a schema the listener already has ("X for Y", a familiar analogy) that leads them to the right behaviour.
   - *Unexpected*: break the listener's prediction on the dimension that matters, then explain why. To hold attention over time, open a question and close it later.
   - *Concrete*: a specific person doing a specific thing, with sensory detail and a named persona instead of "users".
   - *Credible*: details vivid enough to carry their own proof, and numbers turned into human-scale comparisons. Add one decisive example ("if it works here, it works anywhere"), claims the listener can check themselves, and honest anti-authorities (former sufferers rather than experts).
   - *Emotional*: one person instead of a crowd, and the benefit behind the benefit. Lean on identity: what do people like me do here? [Godin]
   - *Stories*: spot real ones rather than inventing them. Challenge plots inspire perseverance, connection plots inspire helping, and creativity plots inspire experimenting. A springboard story shows how a familiar problem could change.
4. **Diagnose by symptom** (a synthesis of [Heath]):
   | Symptom | Fix first |
   |---|---|
   | Nobody pays attention | Unexpected: break a guess tied to the core |
   | They don't understand | Concrete: an example, analogy or persona |
   | They don't believe it | Credible: a checkable claim, vivid detail, an anti-authority |
   | They believe it but don't act | Emotional and Story |
   | Everyone agrees, nothing changes | A simpler core plus a springboard story |
5. **Decide whether the message needs to stick or to spread.** If people need to pass it on, also run the STEPPS audit [Berger] in `word-of-mouth-and-virality`.
6. **Choose the order for the audience** [Heath] [Schwartz]. Put the core first for people who already have context: staff, investors, partners, a market hearing the claim for the first time. For cold or sceptical buyers, open with identification or the mechanism and land the core afterwards (`copywriting-and-headlines`).

## Rules of thumb
- Abstraction is an expert's habit. Go down to the level where everyone can see the actual thing.
- People retell stories and forget statistics, so keep the story when you summarize.
- A surprise must make sense in hindsight. A gimmick wins attention for the wrong idea.
- Don't stretch a charged word ("revolutionary", "seamless") until it means nothing.
- Pure self-interest framing can insult people who act from identity or duty.
- Put the before and after versions side by side, score both, pick one, then test it live.

## Mistakes
- Burying the point under background and caveats.
- Three "key messages". The audience keeps none.
- A slogan that's compact but has no core in it.
- Leading with a statistics slide when you want people to act.
- Polishing delivery while the core is still fuzzy. Polish doesn't predict recall.
- Reducing a story to its moral. A moral can be pulled out of a story, but a story can't be rebuilt from a moral.

## Validate it
- **Assumption created:** `A#: message M is understood and recalled by [segment] and leads to action X [ASSUMPTION, E0]`.
- **Cheapest test:** tell the message to 5 people from the segment who haven't heard it. A day later, ask each one to retell it without prompting.
  - Pass if at least 3 of 5 reproduce the core. That is observed behaviour, E2 for recall.
  - Then put the old and new versions on a live surface (homepage hero, outbound opener, deck slide 1) and count the action you want: replies, sign-ups, next meetings.
- **Evidence bar:**
  - Liking the message is E0.
  - Recall and clicks are E2.
  - Scheduled next steps are E3.
  - Purchases are E4.
  - Log the result in `/facts-assumptions-unknowns`.
