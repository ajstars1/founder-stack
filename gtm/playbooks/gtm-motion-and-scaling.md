# Choose the GTM motion and scale it without stalling
**Use when:** choosing founder-, sales-, product- or partner-led, deciding when to hire reps, when sales, marketing and CS dispute numbers, or planning expansion.  ·  **Sources:** MOVE (Vajre), Crossing the Chasm (Moore), Product-Led Growth (Bush), The Cold Start Problem (Chen)

## Method
1. **Find your stage on the maturity curve.** Revenue alone won't tell you [Vajre]:
   - **Ideation (problem-market fit):** founder-led, lead volume, inefficient by design. The job is to find who buys and why.
   - **Transition (product-market fit):** account and segment focus, prescriptive plays, efficiency starts to matter.
   - **Execution (platform-market fit):** customer-cohort focus, growth from the installed base, several products.
2. **Check the blockers for leaving your stage** [Vajre]:
   - **Leaving ideation is blocked by:** scaling before you know the ideal customer; heavy discounting or renewals at lower prices; no clear main reason for churn; customers unsure of ROI at renewal; selling something you don't deliver; and depending on a few hero sellers.
   - **Leaving transition is blocked by:** a single product; only one way to hit the number; fighting on many fronts and dominating none; teams that can't say no.
3. **Answer the four MOVE questions in order**, and re-answer them each stage [Vajre]:
   - **Market (whom):** narrow from total addressable to best-fit accounts now, then accounts showing intent, then live pipeline. In ideation, learn what "ideal" is before you list.
   - **Operations (how you run):** one scorecard from one data source, owned by a revenue-operations role that sits outside sales, marketing and CS.
   - **Velocity (when to scale):** measure how fast people and customers ramp before adding headcount.
   - **Expansion (where next):** step 7, only after velocity is proven.
4. **Match motion to buyer and channel** [Moore] [Bush]:
   - Enterprise executive → direct sales.
   - End user → self-serve or product-led.
   - Department head → inside or assisted sales.
   - Small business → resellers, agencies or marketplaces.
   - Technical builders → developer-led adoption plus distribution partners.
   Founders sell until a written, repeatable playbook exists (`/sales`).
5. **Measure the right things for the stage** [Vajre]:
   - **Ideation:** by function (activities, leads, conversion, win rate).
   - **Transition:** by segment (pipeline coverage, deal size and velocity, CAC, gross retention).
   - **Execution:** by cohort (lifetime value, time to value, customer ROI, net revenue retention).
6. **Hire on evidence** [Vajre]:
   - Add the next seller when current sellers ramp to target predictably and the playbook is written down.
   - Track ramp ratios: rep to quota, marketing to pipeline, customer to ROI.
   - Before any launch, decide what sales will *stop* doing to make room.
   - A fire is not a hiring plan.
7. **Expand in sequence** [Vajre] [Moore]:
   - **Selling capacity:** direct, then referral partners, then channel and agency programmes.
   - **Coverage:** adjacent segments, regions, or up- or down-market.
   - **Solutions:** from point product to suite.
   Before building, buying or partnering, ask what makes current customers more successful.
8. **Hand over from pioneers to settlers.** Once a segment is won, move early-market builders and sellers to new bets, and give the segment to managers who run repeatable plays [Moore].

## Rules of thumb
- The CEO owns GTM as a process, not just as a strategy deck [Vajre].
- Customer success belongs on the revenue team. Retention is acquisition you don't have to pay for again [Vajre].
- Change the motion before the growth dip arrives. You can't hire your way out of a stall [Vajre].
- A new stage adds motions. It doesn't delete the old ones [Vajre].
- Resold during onboarding? The sales-to-CS hand-off is broken [Vajre].
- Venture growth curves (from about $2M ARR: triple, triple, then double three times) are fundraising benchmarks, not operating plans. Plan a staircase of segments [Chen] [Moore].

## Mistakes
- Hiring a sales team to discover the ideal customer.
- A one-off offsite "GTM strategy" never iterated [Vajre].
- Separate funnels and numbers per function past ideation [Vajre].
- Adding a second product that is really a feature.
- Expanding geographically before the home segment refers business.

## Template
```
Stage: ideation / transition / execution    evidence: ___
Blockers present: ___
M — Market: TAM ___ → best-fit now ___ → in-market signals ___ → pipeline ___
O — Operations: scorecard owner ___ | single source ___ | metrics for stage ___
V — Velocity: rep ramp ___ days to target | customer time-to-ROI ___ | playbook written? __
E — Expansion (only if V proven): selling capacity / coverage / solutions → ___
Motion per buyer: ___ → ___
Next hire: role ___ | trigger met? ___ | what stops to make room ___
```

## Validate it
- **Assumption created:** `A#: A new [role] reaches [target] within [N] weeks using the current playbook [ASSUMPTION, E0]`, or `A#: [motion] produces pipeline at CAC below [X] in [segment]`.
- **Cheapest test:** have a non-founder (a contractor, an early hire or a co-founder who hasn't sold) run the written playbook on 20–30 accounts for a few weeks. Meetings and stage progress you observe are E2. Scheduled pilots or proposals are E3. Closed revenue is E4.
- **Evidence bar:** a second person, not the founder, closes paid deals (E4) at a cost and cycle within your model, across at least 3 deals. Only then add headcount. Log ramp data in `/facts-assumptions-unknowns`.
