# Position the product so the right buyers get it fast
**Use when:** prospects can't say what you sell, you're compared with the wrong rival, cycles are long, buyers push on price, or the product outgrew its first label. Needs a handful of customers who love it.  ·  **Sources:** Obviously Awesome (Dunford), Crossing the Chasm (Moore), Competing Against Luck (Christensen), Play Bigger (Ramadan)

## Method
1. **Start from your best customers.** Pick 5–10 who bought quickly, didn't haggle and referred others. Can't name five? Keep positioning loose and go to `/customer-discovery` [Dunford].
2. **List the real alternatives.** Ask those customers what they would use if you disappeared. Group the answers into 2–5 clusters, including a spreadsheet, a junior hire, an agency, doing nothing. These are your competitors, whatever the founder tracks [Dunford] [Christensen].
3. **Isolate what only you have.** List capabilities those alternatives lack, including ones that look like weaknesses (fewer features can mean faster setup). Attach proof to each (usage data, a customer result, a third-party test); unprovable attributes don't count [Dunford].
4. **Turn attributes into value.** For each one, write the chain capability → what it lets the buyer do → which goal it serves in their situation. Cluster the chains into 1–3 value themes in the buyer's words [Dunford] [Christensen].
5. **Describe who cares most** using traits you can observe from outside: a trigger event, a tool they already run, a rule they must meet, a team shape. Firmographics alone won't do [Dunford] [Moore].
6. **Choose the market frame.** You want the existing category whose built-in assumptions (rivals, must-have features, price range) make your strength obvious. Ask which kinds of products usually have your strengths, then check adjacent growing categories. Customers suggest the obvious frame; treat that as input, not a verdict [Dunford]. Then pick a style:
   - **Head to Head**: only if you lead, or the category is known, leaderless, and you can fund speed.
   - **Big Fish, Small Pond**: the default for challengers. Own a subsegment the leader serves badly, and stay adequate on the category's core criteria.
   - **Create a New Game**: see `category-design.md` [Dunford].
7. **For mainstream B2B buyers, supply the comparison.** Name the *market alternative* whose budget you replace and a *product alternative* built on a similar approach, then stand where they meet. If no existing budget can be named, you are probably not ready for pragmatist buyers [Moore].
8. **Add a trend only if it links product to market.** A trend with no category behind it reads as hype [Dunford].
9. **Turn it into a sales story**: problem, why current options fall short, what an ideal answer does, you in your category, each theme with proof, next step. Copy goes to `/marketing`, the call narrative to `/sales` [Dunford].

## Rules of thumb
- Position on the attributes that win the purchase, not the ones that matter at renewal (support, ease of doing business) [Dunford].
- **Gap test:** after a demo, have a good-fit prospect describe what you do, then compare it with how a happy customer describes you. A mismatch means a positioning problem, not a sales problem [Dunford].
- Size check: if this year's deal target is a large share of every buyer in the segment, the segment is too small. If you couldn't become the obvious reference within it in about a year, it is too broad [Dunford] [Moore].
- Being clear and a bit dull beats being clever and confusing.
- Repositioning changes the roadmap and the price, not only the homepage [Dunford].
- Reopen positioning only when target buyers' perceptions shift (a rival they notice, regulation, technology, the economy). Otherwise hold; review twice a year [Dunford].
- Use the fill-in sentence to check the output, never to do the thinking [Dunford] [Moore].

## Mistakes
- Starting from features or a wished-for segment.
- Benchmarking against startups you follow, not what buyers use.
- Letting marketing "own" positioning without the CEO, product and sales in the room [Dunford] [Ramadan].
- Going head to head with the leader as a newcomer.
- Repositioning whenever a competitor ships.

## Validate it
- **Assumption created:** `A#: Buyers with [traits] file us under [category] and value [theme] over [alternative] [ASSUMPTION, E0]`.
- **Cheapest test:** run the gap test with 5 fresh good-fit prospects. You are looking for E1/E2: they repeat the frame and a theme unprompted, or describe a past purchase in those terms. Then run two outbound sequences or pages differing only in frame; compare meetings booked (E2).
- **Evidence bar:** the frame wins when at least 3 independent good-fit prospects take a scheduled next step under it (E3) and paid conversions or cycle length beat the old frame (E4). If both frames tie, the problem is the segment: go to `beachhead-and-chasm.md`. Design the split in `/experiments`.
