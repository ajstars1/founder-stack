# Price and package the offer
**Use when:** setting a first price, choosing trial vs freemium vs demo, designing tiers, deciding which marketplace side pays, or suspecting you're underpriced.  ·  **Sources:** Product-Led Growth (Bush), Crossing the Chasm (Moore), Platform Revolution (Parker), $100M Offers (Hormozi), The Cold Start Problem (Chen)

## Method
1. **Pick the value metric**, the unit that ties what the customer pays to what they get. It must be easy to grasp, move with the value received, and grow as the customer grows. Find it by comparing best customers with churned ones. Per-seat fits only when value scales with people (collaboration, networks). Prefer outcome-based units where outcomes are measurable [Bush].
2. **Estimate the value.** Add the functional gain (revenue added, cost removed, risk reduced, hours saved), then emotional and social value. Starting near a tenth of total value leaves the buyer an obvious win [Bush].
3. **Find the acceptable range.** Ask target buyers the Van Westendorp questions (too cheap, a bargain, getting expensive, too expensive). With few customers, ask only what feels acceptable and what feels expensive. It's a range hint, never proof [Bush].
4. **Adjust for the buyer type** [Moore]:
   - **Visionaries** accept value-based, project-style pricing.
   - **Pragmatists** compare you with the market leader. A segment leader can hold a modest premium; Moore puts the ceiling around 30%.
   - **Conservatives** expect cost-plus logic.
   Set the price from value with Bush, then sanity-check it against the leader before selling to mainstream buyers [Bush] [Moore].
5. **Choose how buyers first experience it.** The free model is tied to your pricing. Run MOAT [Bush]:
   - **Market strategy.** Dominant (better and far cheaper) suits freemium or a trial. Differentiated (better for a niche at a premium) suits a trial or demo; freemium rarely works. Disruptive (simpler and cheaper for over-served buyers) suits freemium.
   - **Ocean.** A new problem buyers must learn about leans sales-assisted. A crowded, known problem leans product-led.
   - **Audience.** Top-down buyers need a demo or pilot. Bottom-up users need a trial or freemium.
   - **Time-to-value.** Fast value supports free. Slow value needs assistance.
   If unsure, start with a trial; add freemium only once it converts [Bush].
6. **Package in at most three tiers**, named for the buyer each one serves so buyers sort themselves. Put the core value in every tier, bundle the supporting features, and sell niche extras as add-ons. Before freeing a feature, estimate downgrades [Bush].
7. **On platforms, decide whom to charge** [Parker] [Chen]:
   - Never charge to join or list. Charge when value is realised (a transaction fee), or for enhanced access and curation.
   - Subsidize the side the other side values more, the stars, and the most price-sensitive side.
   - A fee can act as a quality filter.
   - Fight leakage with workflow value (payments, protection, records), not hidden contact details.
   - Plan monetization from day one, but delay it on the side you are still trying to attract.
8. **For services, info products and local offers**, raise perceived value before cutting price: bigger dream outcome and confidence, less time-to-result and effort. A narrower niche commands more for the same work. Offer construction (guarantees, bonuses, naming) belongs to `/marketing` [Hormozi].
9. **Review regularly.** Raise prices as proof builds up and grandfather existing customers. Watch ARPU and revenue churn monthly [Bush] [Hormozi].

## Rules of thumb
- Price complaints count only from best-fit buyers.
- There is no prize for being the second-cheapest option [Hormozi].
- Heavy discounting and renewals at lower prices mean the use case is being commoditized. Fix positioning, not price [Vajre].
- Giving away too much kills upgrades. Giving away too little hides the value [Bush].
- Network products should keep entry free or near free. Each fee at the door slows the network [Chen].

## Mistakes
- Cost-plus for near-zero-marginal-cost software [Bush].
- Copying a competitor's price on the assumption that they researched it [Bush].
- Treating survey answers about price as validation.
- Five tiers nobody reads.
- Charging both sides of a marketplace before either side is liquid [Parker].

## Validate it
- **Assumption created:** `A#: [Segment] will pay [price] per [value metric] [ASSUMPTION, E0]`. Per `/facts-assumptions-unknowns`, **pricing claims accept only E4.** Survey and interview answers about price stay E0–E1 at any sample size.
- **Cheapest test:** put the real price on a live checkout or deposit page for targeted traffic, or send real proposals at 2–3 price points to comparable prospects. A signed order or payment is E4. A follow-up booked after seeing the price is E3, only a direction.
- **Evidence bar:** at least 3 independent paid conversions at the price, with retention through the first renewal or repeat purchase. If nobody objects to the price, test higher.
