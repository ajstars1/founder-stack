# Test and build a moat
**Use when:** a funded clone or an incumbent is coming for you, an investor asks what stops others copying you, or you are choosing which advantage to build next.  ·  **Sources:** 7 Powers (Helmer), The Cold Start Problem (Chen), Platform Revolution (Parker), Competing Against Luck (Christensen), Play Bigger (Ramadan)

## Method
1. **List every competitor:** direct rivals, functional substitutes (other ways buyers get the job done, including doing nothing), and potential entrants (incumbents, funded clones, AI-native builds). Power is always measured against a specific competitor [Helmer] [Christensen].
2. **Split each claimed advantage into Benefit and Barrier.** The Benefit is a higher price, lower cost or lower capital need. The Barrier is the reason a competitor can't copy the Benefit away. Examine the Barrier first. Benefits are common; Barriers are rare [Helmer].
3. **Test the seven Powers.** Write "none" when one doesn't apply [Helmer]:
   - **Counter-Positioning:** a new model the incumbent won't copy because copying would hurt its existing business. It can also hold back because of bias against an unproven model, or because the copy would cut someone's bonus. It doesn't protect you from other startups with the same model.
   - **Cornered Resource:** preferential, underpriced access to an asset that adds value on its own: a licence, exclusive data, a patent, a unique supplier. A great team rarely counts, because talent can leave or reprice itself.
   - **Network Economies:** each customer's value rises with the installed base. It is bounded by where the network stops.
   - **Scale Economies:** unit cost falls with volume. You can create it by turning variable costs into fixed ones (owned content, owned infrastructure).
   - **Switching Costs:** money, process (workflows, data, integrations) or relationships lost by leaving. It only protects follow-on sales to existing customers.
   - **Process Power:** organizational know-how that takes years to replicate. Rare and late.
   - **Branding:** buyers pay more for an objectively identical offer. Slow to build and rare in B2B.
4. **Check which window is open now** using the Power Progression [Helmer]:
   - **Before takeoff:** Counter-Positioning and Cornered Resource.
   - **During takeoff:** Network Economies, Scale Economies and Switching Costs. Miss this window and these are very hard to get later.
   - **Stability** (growth below roughly 30–40% a year): Process Power and Branding.
   Pick at most two Powers and one concrete move for each.
5. **Respond to a funded clone** to fit your situation:
   - **If you are counter-positioned against an incumbent:** keep a respectful, low-key tone. Loud claims of superiority speed up their response [Helmer].
   - **In takeoff:** race for relative scale. A rival entering doesn't "validate the market"; it threatens your share [Helmer].
   - **Network rivals:** defend your weakest sub-networks, compete for the hard side, and keep density higher than breadth. A rival's big launch across many places usually creates many weak networks [Chen].
   - **Platforms:** raise the cost of multihoming, use your data advantage, and outdesign the rival [Parker].
6. **Strike out the non-moats:** being first, learning speed without a Barrier, operational excellence, a feature lead, "we use AI", and plain awareness [Helmer] [Chen] [Ramadan].
7. **Build the long game.** Integrate your processes around the customer's job (who owns it end to end, measures of customer progress). Products are copied quickly; well-integrated experiences are not. This pays off with scale, not at seed [Christensen].

## Rules of thumb
- If you can't name what an incumbent would lose by copying you, you have no Counter-Positioning [Helmer].
- If retention doesn't rise with network density, you don't have network effects yet, whatever the pitch deck says [Chen].
- A low network threshold is easy to reach and just as easy for rivals to reach. A high one is harder to reach and harder to attack [Chen].
- Winner-take-all needs Parker's conditions. Without them, plan to share the market [Parker] [Chen].
- Execution can work as a strategy only during takeoff, when imitators can't catch up in time [Helmer].

## Mistakes
- Calling a Benefit (cheaper, faster, better) a moat.
- Claiming network effects for a single-player product.
- Treating the founding team as a Cornered Resource.
- Welcoming a funded entrant during takeoff.
- Pursuing Branding Power at seed stage.

## Validate it
- **Assumption created:** `A#: [Power] protects us against [competitor] because [Barrier] [ASSUMPTION, E0]`.
- **Cheapest tests by Power:**
  - **Switching costs:** see whether customers stay when a rival offers a discount. Observed retention under offer is E2; renewals at full price are E4.
  - **Network effects:** plot retention or price against network density. A visible slope is E2.
  - **Counter-Positioning:** look for the incumbent's public statements and pricing that show reluctance (E2 artifacts).
  - **Cornered Resource:** a signed exclusive is E3; its renewal is E4.
- **Evidence bar:** an independent E2 or better for the Barrier, not just the Benefit. Re-test each time a significant competitor moves. Use `/secondary-research` for competitor facts.
